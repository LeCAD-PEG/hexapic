Electron beam between grounded plates#

A cold electron beam enters an empty box through its left wall, crosses it between two grounded plates and leaves through the right wall. The charge of the beam lowers the potential on its axis and pushes the electrons apart, so the beam widens on its way. The case has an emitter, absorbing conductors and two dielectric walls, and no collisions. The same case is run with XOOPIC, from which it is taken.

Setup#

Domain

2 m × 1 m, 48 × 24 cells of 41.7 mm

Plates

\(y = 0\) and \(y = 1\) m: grounded, absorbing

Left wall

dielectric, absorbing, \(\partial\phi/\partial x = 0\); it keeps the charge it collects

Right wall

dielectric, absorbing, \(\partial\phi/\partial x = 0\); the charge it collects is discarded

Emitter

the left wall between \(y = 0.292\) m and \(y = 0.708\) m

Beam

electrons at \(10^{6}\) m/s along \(x\) (2.84 eV), no thermal spread, 5 µA per metre of depth

Particles

4000 electrons per macroparticle, 39 macroparticles per step, about 15 500 in the steady state

Time step

5 ns: the beam moves 0.12 cells per step

Run

2000 steps (10 µs, five transits of the box), output every 10 steps, no charge smoothing

Field solver

GMGa3, the 2-D multigrid; relative tolerance \(10^{-5}\)

Cost

about 10 s on one NVIDIA A100

The mean electron density of the steady beam in HEXAPIC, and the walls. The stripes are explained under Results.

Running#

python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam --run
python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam

case.py writes runs/xybeam/case.toml and, with --run, runs build/HEXAPIC on it (--hexapic names another binary). case.toml beside the script is the same case, for a run without Python. analyze.py prints the numbers below and writes results.json and the two figures into the run’s directory. Given several runs, it prints their mean and standard deviation.

Option of case.py

--run

run HEXAPIC on the case

--live

write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run

--seed N

the seed of the random numbers (the emitter draws the \(y\) of each electron)

--xoopic

write the case as an XOOPIC deck, case.inp

Results#

Steady state against XOOPIC#

The beam fills the box after one transit, 2 µs. The numbers below are means over the second half of the run. HEXAPIC is run with five seeds:

for s in 1 2 3 4 5; do
    python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam_$s --seed $s --run
done
python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam_?

The rms width \(\sigma_y\) of the beam is taken in each column of cells, from the number of electrons in its cells. A beam of the emitter’s width \(w\) and uniform density has \(\sigma_y = w/\sqrt{12} = 0.1203\) m.

The beam widens from 0.120 m to 0.175 m in both codes, and from the middle of the box to the exit the potentials agree within 0.3 %.

The two codes differ at the emitter. XOOPIC starts each electron on the wall and moves it by a random part of a time step. HEXAPIC starts every electron half a cell inside the box, at \(x = 20.8\) mm. The beam of HEXAPIC is therefore half a cell shorter, which is 1.0 % of the box: it holds 0.8 % fewer electrons and 1.0 % less kinetic energy. The first half cell holds no charge, so the potential at the left wall is 6 % higher, and the field energy is 1.7 % lower. The electrons of one step also start at the same \(x\) and stay together. A column of cells holds the electrons of 8.33 steps, so some columns hold eight groups and some nine. These are the stripes in the density above, and the ripple in the width of HEXAPIC near the exit.

The field energy of XOOPIC still rises by 0.4 % over the second half of the run. That of HEXAPIC is constant within 0.2 %. The cause was not looked for.

XOOPIC’s numbers come from its stored run in reference/xoopic/. analyze.py --xoopic DIR takes them from a new run of XOOPIC instead (see reference/SOURCE.md).

The script#

case.py
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""xybeam: a cold electron beam crosses a box between two grounded plates and spreads by its own space charge.

After XOOPIC's deck input/xybeam.inp (reference/SOURCE.md), as a PICMI script: one description
of the case for HEXAPIC and for XOOPIC.

    python case.py DIR              # DIR/case.toml
    python case.py DIR --run        # and HEXAPIC on it (build/HEXAPIC; --hexapic: another), into DIR/output
    python case.py DIR --xoopic     # DIR/case.inp, the same case as an XOOPIC deck (--run: XOOPIC on it)
    python case.py                  # the case.toml beside this script (--xoopic: reference/xoopic/case.inp)

A box of 2 m x 1 m, empty at the start.  The plates at y = 0 and y = 1 m are
grounded and absorb.  The walls at x = 0 and x = 2 m are dielectric: they
absorb, and the potential has no normal gradient there (the left one keeps
the charge it collects, the right one discards it).  The middle 0.417 m of
the left wall emits electrons at 1e6 m/s along x, 5 uA per metre of depth,
with no thermal spread.  There are no collisions and no magnetic field.  The
beam's own charge lowers the potential on its axis by 0.11 V and widens it on
its way to the right wall, where it leaves.

--seed N is the run's seed (the emitter draws each electron's y at random),
--live writes the frames HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run.  The XOOPIC deck
also has a Diagnostic block that writes the potential beside the phase files
(analyze.py reads both).
"""

import argparse
from pathlib import Path

ap = argparse.ArgumentParser(description="the electron beam between grounded plates: case.toml, or XOOPIC's deck")
ap.add_argument("dir", nargs="?", type=Path)
ap.add_argument("--xoopic", action="store_true")
ap.add_argument("--seed", type=int)
ap.add_argument("--live", action="store_true")
ap.add_argument("--run", action="store_true")
ap.add_argument("--hexapic", type=Path, help="the binary --run runs (default build/HEXAPIC)")
args = ap.parse_args()
HERE = Path(__file__).resolve().parent

if args.xoopic:
    from xoopic import picmi
else:
    from hexapic import picmi

NX, NY = 48, 24                 # the cells: 2 m x 1 m
DX = 1.0 / NY                   # their size [m]
BEAM = (7, 17)                  # the emitting nodes of the left wall: y = 0.292 .. 0.708 m
CURRENT = 5e-6                  # the beam's current [A per metre of depth]
SPEED = 1e6                     # the electrons' speed [m/s]: 2.84 eV
WEIGHT = 4000.0                 # electrons per macroparticle: 39 macroparticles a step
TRANSITS = 5                    # the run's length in transit times of the box
ME, QE = 9.11e-31, 1.6e-19      # the electron as XOOPIC's deck rounds it [kg, C]
DT = 5e-9                       # the time step [s]: the beam moves 0.12 cells a step
EVERY = 10                      # the output's period [steps]

lx, ly = NX * DX, NY * DX
steps = round(TRANSITS * lx / SPEED / DT)

grid = picmi.Cartesian2DGrid(number_of_cells=[NX, NY], lower_bound=[0.0, 0.0], upper_bound=[lx, ly],
                             lower_boundary_conditions=[None, None], upper_boundary_conditions=[None, None])
sim = picmi.Simulation(solver=picmi.ElectrostaticSolver(grid=grid), time_step_size=DT, max_steps=steps,
                       hexapic_run=None if args.seed is None else {"seed": args.seed},
                       hexapic_output={"phase_space": False} | ({"live": True} if args.live else {}),
                       hexapic_smoothing={"x_passes": 0, "y_passes": 0},   # a comparison: no charge smoothing
                       xoopic_electrostatic_flag=1,                        # the deck's Poisson solver
                       hexapic_title="xybeam",
                       hexapic_description=("A cold electron beam crosses a box between two grounded plates and "
                                            "spreads by its own space charge. The walls the beam enters and "
                                            "leaves through are dielectric. After XOOPIC's input/xybeam.inp, "
                                            "with 48 x 24 cells of 1/24 m (20 x 20 of 1/20 m there)."))

electrons = picmi.Species(name="electrons", mass=ME, charge=-QE)
sim.add_species(electrons, None)

sim.add_boundary(picmi.Boundary(type="dielectric", location="x_lo", permittivity=1.0))
sim.add_boundary(picmi.Boundary(type="dielectric", location="x_hi", permittivity=1.0, charge_accumulating=False))
sim.add_boundary(picmi.Boundary(type="grounded", location="y_lo"))
sim.add_boundary(picmi.Boundary(type="grounded", location="y_hi"))
sim.add_beam(picmi.Beam(species="electrons", nodes={"from": [0, BEAM[0]], "to": [0, BEAM[1]]}, current=CURRENT,
                        weight=WEIGHT, drift=[SPEED, 0.0, 0.0], xoopic_block="EmitPort"))
sim.add_diagnostic(picmi.FieldDiagnostic(grid=grid, period=EVERY))


def mesh_diagnostic(deck, name):
    """Adds the Diagnostic block that makes XOOPIC write a mesh (phi, rho, ...) beside its phase files."""
    block = ["Diagnostic", "{", f"  VarName = {name}", "  directory = output", f"  file = {name}_%04d.dat",
             "  append = 0", "  n_start = 0", f"  n_step = {EVERY}", "  n_end = 0", "}", "}"]
    deck.write_text(deck.read_text().rstrip()[:-1] + "\n".join(block) + "\n")     # before the Region's closing brace


if args.run and args.dir is None:
    ap.error("--run needs a directory for the run")
out = args.dir or (HERE / "reference/xoopic" if args.xoopic else HERE)
case = sim.write_input_file(out / ("case.inp" if args.xoopic else "case.toml"))
if args.xoopic:
    mesh_diagnostic(case, "phi")
    if args.dir is not None:
        (out / "output").mkdir(exist_ok=True)       # XOOPIC does not make its Diagnostic files' directory
print(case)
if args.run:
    sim.run(**({} if args.xoopic or args.hexapic is None else {"hexapic": args.hexapic.resolve()}))

References#

The case follows XOOPIC’s deck input/xybeam.inp [Verboncoeur et al., 1995]. That deck has a box of 1 m × 1 m with 20 × 20 cells and an emitter of 0.4 m. Here the box is 2 m × 1 m with 48 × 24 cells of 1/24 m, and the emitter is 0.417 m wide (the nodes 7 to 17). The beam, the walls and the time step are those of the deck. There is no theory in this example.

XOOPIC’s run (xoopic/)#

  • case.inp: the deck XOOPIC ran, as python case.py --xoopic writes it. Besides the case it has two Diagnostic blocks, which make XOOPIC write the electrons’ phase space and the potential every 10 steps.

  • history.csv: XOOPIC’s field energy, kinetic energy (J/m) and number of macroparticles at every 10th of 2000 steps, from the ENERGY_CSV lines it prints.

  • profiles.csv: the steady state, the mean over the steps 1000 to 1990: the rms width of the beam in each column of cells (from the phase-space files) and the potential on the axis \(y = 0.5\) m (from the potential files).

XOOPIC 2.70 (github.com/rinku-mishra/xoopic, commit 3fa6670) with the ENERGY_CSV lines added to otools/diagn.cpp (Running XOOPIC gives the patch and the build), run on 2026-11-16 as

mkdir -p runs/xybeam_xoopic
python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam_xoopic --xoopic --run > runs/xybeam_xoopic/xoopic.log

which is xoopic -nox -i case.inp -s 2000 -exit in that directory, about 6 s on one CPU core. The two CSV files were then written by

python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam_? --xoopic runs/xybeam_xoopic --store

XOOPIC draws the same random numbers in each run.
