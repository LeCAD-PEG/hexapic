# Floating wall

A wall that is not connected to anything collects the electrons and ions
that reach it. The electrons are faster, so the wall charges negatively
until it repels most of them, and the two fluxes are equal. The potential
then drops by a few $T_e/e$ over a thin layer in front of the wall, the
sheath, in which the plasma is not neutral. The case tests the floating
wall, the volumetric source and the heating, and it compares the sheath
with the standard formula.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | $L = 25.2$ mm along $x$, 2.1 mm along $y$ (periodic) |
| Grid | 96 × 8 cells of 0.263 mm, half a Debye length at $10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and 5 eV; the problem is 1-D along $x$ |
| Walls | reflective at $x = 0$ (the plane of symmetry of the plasma), floating at $x = L$ |
| Plasma | electrons and H⁺ with the real mass ratio; at the start $10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, 1000 macro-particles per cell and species, the electrons on the ions' positions |
| Source | electron–ion pairs in $0 < x < L/2$ at a mean rate of $8.7\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}$, falling as $\cos(\pi x / L)$ to zero at $L/2$; electrons at 5 eV, ions at 0.5 eV |
| Heating | in $0 < x < L/2$ a tenth of the electrons is drawn anew from a Maxwellian of $T_e = 5$ eV every step (`model = "resample"`) |
| Collisions | none; between $L/2$ and the wall there is neither a source nor heating |
| Time step | $1.12\times10^{-10}$ s ($\omega_{pe}\,\Delta t = 0.2$ at $10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$) |
| Run | 133 900 steps: 13 times $L/c_s$, the time of an ion at the sound speed across the domain (1.15 µs); output every 206 steps over the last 6, no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid |
| Cost | about 150 s on one NVIDIA A100 |

The heating is needed. Only electrons with enough energy along $x$ reach
the wall, and without collisions nothing replaces them: the heating in the
source region keeps the electrons Maxwellian there.

## Running

```bash
python examples/walls/floating_wall/case.py runs/floating_wall
build/HEXAPIC runs/floating_wall/case.toml > runs/floating_wall/run.log
python examples/walls/floating_wall/analyze.py runs/floating_wall
```

`case.py` writes `runs/floating_wall/case.toml`; with `--run` it also runs
`build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml` beside
the script is the same case. `analyze.py` reads the profiles along $x$ that
the run writes at every output step (`xprof_<step>.0`) and the log of the
run, `run.log`: its `[WallFlux]` lines have the numbers of ions and
electrons that the wall absorbed. It prints the numbers below and writes
`results.json` and the figure into the run's directory. `--live` makes
`case.py` write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run.

## Results

The numbers are means over the last six transit times, over which the
number of particles changes by 0.3 %. The scatter is the standard
deviation of six values, one from the mean profiles of each transit time.

The sheath edge $x_s$ is defined here as the point where the charge
separation $(n_i - n_e)/n_i$, followed from the wall into the plasma, falls
to 2 %. The electron temperature $T_e$ at the edge is the mean of the
temperatures of the three directions. The ion temperature $T_i$ is that
along $x$, and $u_i$ is the mean velocity of the ions.

![The potential and the densities of ions and electrons against x: the potential falls slowly in the source region, stays flat and drops by 13 V in the sheath at the wall](../../_examples/walls/floating_wall/figures/sheath.png)

*Left: the potential, with the plasma at $x = 0$ as zero, the sheath edge,
and the wall's potential that the formula gives from the sheath edge.
Right: the densities of the ions and the electrons.*

| | HEXAPIC |
|---|---|
| Sheath edge, distance from the wall [mm] | $5.5 \pm 0.2$ |
| $T_e$ at the edge [eV] | $4.88 \pm 0.01$ |
| $T_i$ at the edge [eV] | $0.111 \pm 0.002$ |
| Density at the edge, of that at $x = 0$ | 0.41 |
| Drop from $x = 0$ to the wall [V] | $17.69 \pm 0.05$, which is $3.54\,T_e/e$ |
| Net current into the wall, of the ion current | $3\times10^{-5}$ |

The wall absorbed 2 309 575 macro-ions and 2 309 497 macro-electrons over
the six transit times: its net current is zero within $10^{-4}$ of the ion
current.

### Sheath drop against theory

Maxwellian electrons of the temperature $T_e$ reach a wall that is
$\Delta\phi$ below the sheath edge with the flux
$n_s \sqrt{T_e / 2\pi m_e}\,\exp(-e\Delta\phi/T_e)$, where $n_s$ is the
density at the edge. Ions that enter the sheath at the sound speed
$c_s = \sqrt{(T_e + T_i)/m_i}$ carry the flux $n_s c_s$. The two fluxes are
equal for

$$
\frac{e\,\Delta\phi}{T_e} = \frac{1}{2}\ln\!\left[\frac{m_i}{2\pi m_e}\,\frac{1}{1 + T_i/T_e}\right] .
$$

| | $\Delta\phi$ [V] | $e\,\Delta\phi / T_e$ |
|---|---|---|
| HEXAPIC | $13.04 \pm 0.05$ | $2.67 \pm 0.01$ |
| Formula, with the measured $T_e$ and $T_i$ | $13.80 \pm 0.03$ | 2.83 |

The measured drop is 5.5 % below the formula. The formula assumes that the
ions enter exactly at the sound speed. Here they are 27 % faster (next
section), and their flux is larger by this factor: the same balance then
gives a drop that is smaller by $T_e \ln(u_i/c_s)$, 12.63 V, and the measured
drop is 3 % above it. The theory does not support a closer comparison than
10 % to 20 %: the edge of the sheath is not sharp, and the electrons at the
edge are not Maxwellian, because those that would return from the wall with
a high energy are missing. Their temperature along $x$ is 4.68 eV there.

The result depends on the definition of the edge:

| Edge where $(n_i - n_e)/n_i$ is | 1 % | 2 % | 5 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distance from the wall [mm] | 6.3 | 5.5 | 4.4 |
| $\Delta\phi$, HEXAPIC [V] | 13.18 | 13.04 | 12.66 |
| $\Delta\phi$, formula [V] | 13.82 | 13.80 | 13.80 |
| $u_i / c_s$ | 1.25 | 1.27 | 1.34 |

The drop between the plasma at $x = 0$ and the sheath edge, 4.6 V, is the
presheath. It depends on the source and is not compared with a theory here.

### Ion speed against the Bohm criterion

A sheath in front of a negative wall exists only if the ions enter it at
least at the sound speed (Bohm's criterion),

$$
u_i \ge c_s = \sqrt{\frac{T_e + T_i}{m_i}} .
$$

| At the sheath edge | |
|---|---|
| HEXAPIC, $u_i$ [km/s] | $27.8 \pm 0.1$ |
| Sound speed $c_s$, with the measured $T_e$ and $T_i$ [km/s] | $21.87 \pm 0.02$ |
| $u_i / c_s$ | $1.27 \pm 0.01$ |

The ions pass the sound speed at the end of the source region and reach the
sheath edge at $1.27\,c_s$. The criterion is met, but not with the equality
that the formula for the drop assumes.

## References

The potential drop of a planar collisionless sheath in front of a floating
wall and the condition on the ions' speed at the sheath edge (Bohm's
criterion): {cite:t}`riemann1991` and {cite:t}`stangeby2000`. The
formula of the README follows from the equality of the electrons' and the
ions' fluxes into the wall; `analyze.py` gives the derivation in its
docstring.

The case takes no numbers from these sources, and it has no data from other
codes. The drop between the source region and the sheath edge (the
presheath) depends on the source and is not compared with a theory here.
