Solvers#

Every step solves the Poisson equation

(1)#\[ -\nabla\cdot\left(\varepsilon\,\nabla\phi\right) = \rho \]

for the potential \(\phi\). The charge density \(\rho\) comes from the particles, and the permittivity \(\varepsilon\) describes the domain and its dielectrics.

HEXAPIC’s field solvers are its own. They are written entirely in alpaka3 kernels and use no vendor libraries. A solver runs inside the PIC step, on the same domain decomposition as the particles, and computes in its own precision, HEXAPIC_MG_REAL_TYPE, which is double by default. The settings are in [solver].

A build with HYPRE (HEXAPIC_USE_HYPRE) adds type = "hypre" in 2-D. It is meant for A/B checks of the solvers above.

Choosing a solver#

The default, type = "auto", takes the strip solver for a 2-D case that meets its conditions and has at most 128 cells across y, where it is faster than the multigrid. Every other case gets the multigrid. The run prints the choice and its reason. This is the line of the two-stream example, which is periodic in x:

[solver] auto: the multigrid: x is not Dirichlet (Conductor, Equipotential or open) at both ends

The operator#

The operator is the standard five-point stencil on a uniform grid, or the seven-point stencil in 3-D. A permittivity that varies in space is stored at every node. A uniform permittivity is detected at setup and handled by a constant-coefficient path. Every multigrid level uses the same operator, discretised again with the spacing of that level.

Conductors and dielectrics#

The multigrid solvers handle conductors and dielectrics inside the domain.

Biased conductors, both walls and regions, are nodes fixed at their potential. A coarse node is fixed if its prolongation reaches any fixed fine node. A conductor that is only one node thick is therefore present on every level.

Floating conductor regions take the potential that follows from their charge. Each solve combines a solve with the conductors grounded and the unit responses of the conductors, weighted through their capacitance matrix. Regions of one group share one potential.

Floating walls are Neumann walls. The charge they collect enters the right-hand side, and the wall floats to its sheath potential.

Dielectric walls that collect charge are treated in the same way. A node that a biased wall or a conductor also covers keeps its fixed potential.
