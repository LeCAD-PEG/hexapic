# Ion optics of a gridded ion thruster

A gridded ion thruster draws ions out of a discharge plasma through the
holes of two grids. The screen grid is at the high potential of the plasma.
The accel grid behind it is negative: the field between the two grids
accelerates the ions and focuses them into one beamlet per hole, and the
negative potential keeps the electrons that neutralize the beam from
streaming back into the discharge. This case simulates one periodic cell of
such grids in 3-D, with the plasma, two beamlets and the neutralizing
electrons. It has no theory and no other code to compare with, so the
results below describe what the run gives.

## Setup

The beam goes along $x$. The holes sit on a hexagonal lattice with a pitch
of 2.24 mm from hole to hole. The cell is one pitch along $y$ and
$\sqrt{3}$ pitches along $z$, with a hole at its corners and one at its
centre, so it holds two beamlets.

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 9.56 mm × 2.24 mm × 3.88 mm, periodic in $y$ and $z$ |
| Grid | 256 × 60 × 104 cells of 37.3 µm (1.26 Debye lengths of the discharge plasma) |
| Wall at $x = 0$ | reflective: the middle of the discharge chamber |
| Discharge plasma | electrons (4 eV) and Ar⁺ (0.1 eV), $2.5\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, loaded from $x = 0$ to 1.2 mm. Between $x = 0$ and 0.8 mm both are created at the rate that replaces a Bohm flux to the plane of the screen grid, and the electrons are redrawn at 4 eV. |
| Screen grid | from $x = 1.2$ to 1.6 mm, at +1100 V, with holes of 1.91 mm diameter |
| Accel grid | from $x = 2.2$ to 2.72 mm, at −180 V, with holes of 1.14 mm diameter; the gap between the grids is 0.6 mm |
| Grids as objects | conductor boxes, 144 in all: a node is solid where it is farther than the radius of the hole from the axis of every hole |
| Wall at $x = 9.56$ mm | grounded and absorbing. It emits the neutralizing electrons (2 eV) at 0.87 mA, twice the ion current that a Bohm flux carries through the two screen holes. |
| Collisions | Monte Carlo collisions of the electrons and the ions with a background of argon, $3\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ at 300 K. They include the charge exchange of the beam ions. |
| Particles | 100 per cell and species in the discharge plasma at the start, $2\times10^{7}$ per species. The ions are moved every 10th step. |
| Time step | $7.09\times10^{-12}$ s ($\omega_{pe}\,\Delta t = 0.2$) |
| Run | 150 000 steps (1.06 µs), output every 2000 steps |
| Field solver | [GMG3D](../../docs/solvers/gmg3d.md), the 3-D multigrid; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Build | the default build (quadratic particle shape, single precision) |
| Cost | about 70 min on one NVIDIA A100 |

## Running

```bash
python examples/thrusters/ion_thruster/case.py runs/ion_thruster
build/HEXAPIC runs/ion_thruster/case.toml > runs/ion_thruster/run.log
python examples/thrusters/ion_thruster/analyze.py runs/ion_thruster
```

`case.py` writes `runs/ion_thruster/case.toml`. `analyze.py` reads the
series and the log of the run, `run.log`, which has the currents that the
grids collect. It prints the numbers below and writes `results.json` and
the three figures into the run's directory.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--ny N` | cells across a pitch, a multiple of 4 (default 60). It sets the cell size, and with it the cells along $x$ and $z$. |
| `--steps N` | the number of steps |
| `--every N` | an output every `N` steps |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |

| Option of `analyze.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--last N` | take the means of the currents over the last `N` outputs (default: the last third) |
| `--log FILE` | the log of the run (default `run.log` in the run's directory) |

## Results

The beam reaches the far wall after about 100 ns, and the currents settle
over the next 600 ns. The maps and the profiles are from the last output,
at 1.06 µs. The currents are means over the last third of the outputs, from
0.72 to 1.06 µs, with the standard error of the outputs.

### Potential and beamlets

![The potential and the ion density in a plane through a hole of both grids](../../_examples/thrusters/ion_thruster/figures/maps.png)

*The potential (top) and the density of the ions (bottom) at 1.06 µs in the
plane through the centre hole, $y$ at half a pitch. The grids are grey: the
screen grid on the left, the accel grid on the right. The black line is the
contour of 0 V.*

The discharge plasma is at 1116 V, 16 V above the screen grid. A sheath
separates it from the grid, and its edge bulges into each screen hole. The
ions that cross this edge are accelerated towards the accel grid and
focused into a beamlet, which is narrower than the accel hole and widens
behind it. On the axis of a hole the potential falls to −52 V at
$x = 2.8$ mm, just behind the accel grid. This negative barrier stops the
neutralizing electrons: their density falls to zero near $x = 3.4$ mm, and
the electrons of the discharge end at the screen grid. Further downstream
the electrons balance the charge of the ions, both at
$5\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ in the mean over the cross-section, and
the potential stays below 50 V.

![Profiles along x of the potential, of the densities of the three species and of the ion current](../../_examples/thrusters/ion_thruster/figures/profiles.png)

*Along $x$: the potential on the axis of a hole and its mean over $y$ and
$z$, the mean densities of the three species at 1.06 µs, and the net ion
current through the planes of cells as the mean over the window. The grey
bands are the two grids.*

### Currents and perveance

![The ion currents arriving at the screen grid, in the beam and collected by the two grids over time](../../_examples/thrusters/ion_thruster/figures/currents.png)

*The ion current that arrives at the screen grid, the beam current 1 mm
before the far wall, and the ion currents that the screen grid and the
accel grid collect.*

| Mean over 0.72 to 1.06 µs | |
|---|---|
| Ions arriving at the screen grid [mA] | $0.700 \pm 0.002$ |
| Ions collected by the screen grid [mA] | $0.174 \pm 0.001$ |
| Beam current, 1 mm before the far wall [mA] | $0.541 \pm 0.003$ |
| Beam current per hole [mA] | 0.270 |
| Ion transparency of the screen grid | 0.77 |
| Ions collected by the accel grid [mA] | $1.8\times10^{-5}$ |

Of the ions that arrive at the screen grid, 77 % leave as the beam. The
open area of the screen grid is 66 % of its plane: the sheath edge that
bulges into the holes also draws in ions that started in front of the solid
part. The accel grid collects $3\times10^{-5}$ of the beam current, so the
beamlets pass it without touching it, and the slow ions of the charge
exchange add little at this gas density. The beam current still falls
slowly over the window, from 0.57 to 0.52 mA.

The perveance of a hole is $P = I / V^{3/2}$, with the beam current $I$ of
the hole and the voltage $V$ from the plasma to the accel grid, 1296 V. The
Child–Langmuir law limits it to

$$
P_\mathrm{max} = \frac{4 \varepsilon_0}{9} \sqrt{\frac{2e}{M}}\, \frac{\pi r_s^2}{l_e^2} ,
\qquad l_e^2 = (l_g + t_s)^2 + r_s^2 ,
$$

where $r_s$ is the radius of the screen hole, $t_s$ the thickness of the
screen grid, $l_g$ the gap and $M$ the mass of the ion. The run gives
$P = 5.79\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{A/V^{3/2}}$, which is 0.45 of
$P_\mathrm{max} = 1.28\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{A/V^{3/2}}$: the grids work
below their space-charge limit, as the focused beamlets show.

### The beam

The energy and the angles are those of the beam ions between 0.5 and
1.5 mm before the far wall at the last step, $3.5\times10^{5}$
macroparticles.

| | |
|---|---|
| Mean energy [eV] | 1111.6, standard deviation 3.0 |
| Divergence, rms angle to $x$ [°] | 8.8 |
| Angle that holds 95 % of the ions [°] | 16.7 |
| Slow ions among them | 0.06 % |

The ions arrive with the energy of the fall from the plasma at 1116 V to
the beam, which is a few volts above the grounded wall. The slow ions are
those of the charge exchange.

## References

The case has no data from other codes to compare with.

The grids have the dimensions of the script's header: screen holes of
1.91 mm, accel holes of 1.14 mm, a pitch of 2.24 mm, a gap of 0.60 mm, and
grids of 0.40 and 0.52 mm. They are rounded values in the range of the
grids of the NSTAR 30 cm ion thruster and are not taken from a table of a
paper. The gas is argon and the plasma density is chosen for the cell size,
so the case does not model that thruster.

The Child–Langmuir law of the space-charge-limited current, to which
`analyze.py` compares the perveance of a hole: {cite:t}`child1911`,
{cite:t}`langmuir1913`. The Bohm flux that sets the rate of the plasma
source: {cite:t}`riemann1991`. The collisions with the argon background:
[Collisions](../../docs/implementation/collisions.md).
