# Two-stream instability

<video class="hx-video" controls loop muted playsinline preload="metadata" poster="../../_static/videos/two_stream.jpg" src="../../_static/videos/two_stream.mp4"></video>

Two cold electron beams of equal density move through each other in a
periodic box, over a neutralising background of ions. The configuration is
unstable: a perturbation of the charge density grows exponentially until the
electrons are trapped in the wave's potential, and the field energy saturates.
The growth rate is known analytically, which makes the case a test of the
particle push, the charge deposition and the field solve together.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 6.786 µm × 0.212 µm, periodic in $x$ and $y$ |
| Grid | 256 × 8 cells of 26.5 nm; the problem is 1-D along $x$ |
| Electrons | two beams, $5\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ each, drift $\pm10^{7}$ m/s along $x$, thermal speed $3\times10^{4}$ m/s |
| Ions | He⁺, $10^{24}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, thermal speed $3\times10^{3}$ m/s, moved every 10th step |
| Particles | 4 per cell in each beam and in the ions; the beams start on the ions' positions |
| Frequencies | $\omega_{pe} = 5.64\times10^{13}$ rad/s for both beams together, $\omega_b = \omega_{pe}/\sqrt{2}$ for one |
| Time step | $5.62\times10^{-16}$ s ($\omega_{pe}\,\Delta t = 0.032$) |
| Run | 2000 steps ($\omega_{pe} t = 63$), output every 10 steps, no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid, as `type = "auto"` chooses for a periodic case; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Cost | about 6 s on one NVIDIA A100 |

## Running

```bash
python examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/case.py runs/two_stream --run
python examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/analyze.py runs/two_stream
```

`case.py` writes `runs/two_stream/case.toml` and, with `--run`, runs
`build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml` beside
the script is the same case, for a run without Python: `build/HEXAPIC
examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/case.toml -d runs/two_stream/output`.
`analyze.py` prints the numbers below and writes `results.json` and the
figure into the run's directory.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--run` | run HEXAPIC on the case |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |
| `--random-load` | load every species at random positions, as XOOPIC does |
| `--seed N` | the seed of the random numbers |
| `--xoopic` | write the case as an XOOPIC deck, `case.inp` |

## Results

### Growth rate against theory

For two cold beams with the speeds $\pm v_b$, a mode with the wave number
$k$ grows when $k v_b < \sqrt{2}\,\omega_b$, at the rate $\gamma$ given by

$$
\gamma^2 = \omega_b \sqrt{\omega_b^2 + 4 k^2 v_b^2} - \omega_b^2 - k^2 v_b^2 .
$$

The largest rate is $\gamma = \omega_b / 2$, at $k v_b = (\sqrt{3}/2)\,\omega_b$.
The box holds the modes $k = 2\pi n / L_x$, and the fastest of them is $n = 4$
with $\gamma = 0.497\,\omega_b$. `analyze.py` takes the amplitude of each mode
from the Fourier transform of $E_x$ and fits its growth rate over the three
e-folds before saturation.

![The amplitudes of the modes 3, 4 and 5 of the electric field against time, with the slope of the theory](../../_examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/figures/growth_rate.png)

*The amplitudes of the three fastest modes. The black line has the slope of
the theory and is shown over the interval of the fit.*

| Mode $n$ | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| HEXAPIC, $\gamma / \omega_b$ | 0.500 | 0.494 | 0.409 |
| Theory, $\gamma / \omega_b$ | 0.479 | 0.497 | 0.425 |

Over six seeds the fastest mode grows at $(0.494 \pm 0.008)\,\omega_b$. The test
`two_stream` in `tests/physics/run.py` requires 5 %.

The measurement needs the beams to start on the ions' positions. The charge
density is then zero at the start, and the instability grows from the small
noise of the thermal spread over about seven e-folds. With every species
loaded at random, the field energy starts only about 25 times below its
saturated value, and there is no linear phase to fit.

### Field energy against XOOPIC

XOOPIC loads its particles at random, so this comparison uses
`--random-load`. The history of the field energy then depends on the seed as
much as on the code, and XOOPIC's run is compared with six seeds of HEXAPIC:

```bash
for s in 1 2 3 4 5 6; do
    python examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/case.py runs/two_stream_$s --random-load --seed $s --run
done
python examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/analyze.py runs/two_stream_?
```

![The field energy against time: XOOPIC's run inside the range of six HEXAPIC seeds](../../_examples/basic_plasma/two_stream/figures/field_energy.png)

*The field energy of XOOPIC's run and the range of six HEXAPIC runs with
different seeds.*

| | HEXAPIC, 6 seeds | XOOPIC |
|---|---|---|
| Saturation, $\omega_{pe} t$ | $11.5 \pm 2.0$ | 10.6 |
| Field energy at saturation [J/m] | $(1.79 \pm 0.35)\times10^{-5}$ | $1.82\times10^{-5}$ |
| Mean field energy over the second half [J/m] | $(4.6 \pm 0.8)\times10^{-6}$ | $3.8\times10^{-6}$ |

XOOPIC's numbers lie within about one standard deviation of HEXAPIC's seeds.
They come from its stored run in `reference/xoopic/`.

### XOOPIC's run (`xoopic/`)

- `case.inp`: the deck XOOPIC ran, as `python case.py --xoopic` writes it
  (the random load). `tests/physics/run.py` fails when `case.py` writes
  another one: the energies below would then be from another case.
- `energy.csv`: XOOPIC's field and kinetic energy (J/m) at every 5th of 2000
  steps, from the `ENERGY_CSV` lines it prints. XOOPIC 2.70
  (github.com/rinku-mishra/xoopic, 3fa6670) with those lines added to
  `otools/diagn.cpp` ([Running XOOPIC](../../docs/verification/codes.md#running-xoopic) gives the patch and the build), run as `xoopic -nox -i case.inp -s 2000 -exit` on 2026-11-16.
  XOOPIC draws the same load each run.
