# Collisions

Two kinds of collisions act after the push. Monte Carlo collisions describe
charged particles colliding with a neutral gas. Coulomb collisions describe
charged particles colliding with each other. Both are set in
[`[[collisions]]`](../user-guide/case-toml.md#collisions).

## Monte Carlo collisions

Electrons and ions collide with a neutral gas. The gas is either a uniform
background with a given pressure and temperature, or a species of neutral
particles, selected with `neutral`.

| Projectile | Processes |
|---|---|
| electrons | elastic, excitation, ionization, and double ionization for He, N and Ne |
| ions | elastic, charge exchange |

Collision candidates are selected with the null-collision method
{cite:p}`vahedi1995`. The upper bound on the collision frequency is computed
for each bin of particles, not once for the whole domain. The energy of an
ion is taken relative to the gas.

The processes are handled as follows:

- In an elastic collision the particle is scattered isotropically. For ions
  this happens in the centre-of-mass frame.
- In an ionization with a neutral species, the new electron–ion pair is made
  from a neutral particle, and that particle is removed.
- In a charge exchange, the ion and the neutral swap velocities.

With `set = "bit1"`, the scattering angles, the energy losses and the energy
sharing follow BIT1. See
[Collision data](../user-guide/collision-data.md#the-bit1-set).

## Coulomb collisions

The charged particles of a cell are paired at random. The pairs are formed
within one species or between two species. Each pair is then scattered with
one of two models:

- `model = "nanbu"` uses the model of Nanbu {cite:p}`nanbu1997`. A single
  scattering angle stands for the many small-angle collisions that happen
  during the collision interval.
- `model = "ta"` uses the binary model of Takizuka and Abe
  {cite:p}`takizuka1977`. Between two different species this is the only
  model available.

For a pair with relative speed $g$ and reduced mass $\mu$, the Nanbu
parameter is

$$
s = \frac{(q_a q_b)^2\, n\, \Delta t_c \ln\Lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0^2\,\mu^2 g^3},
$$

where $n$ is the density of the collision partners and $\Delta t_c$ is the
collision interval. The Coulomb logarithm is computed in each cell as
$\ln(\lambda_D / b_0)$ with
$b_0 = \lvert q_a q_b\rvert / (4\pi\varepsilon_0 \mu g^2)$. You can also fix
it with `log_lambda`.

### The collision interval

Coulomb collisions are applied every `every` steps, and each application
covers that many time steps. If `every` is not set, each pair chooses its own
interval. The interval is chosen so that $s$ at the thermal speed stays
below `s_target`, which is 0.1 by default. It never exceeds `every_max`
steps. With `plasma_period_fraction`, it also stays below that
fraction of the plasma period.
