# Capacitive discharge in helium (Turner benchmark)

Helium between two parallel plates, one grounded and one driven at
13.56 MHz, forms a capacitively coupled discharge: the oscillating sheaths at
the plates heat the electrons, the electrons ionise the gas, and the ions
fall through the sheaths to the plates. {cite:t}`turner2013` define four
such cases of increasing pressure, with every physical and numerical
parameter prescribed, and show that five independent PIC-MCC codes give the
same ion density profile to within their statistical noise. This example
runs the benchmark with HEXAPIC. Case 1, the lowest pressure, is the main
result; HEXAPIC does not reproduce it, and the cause is identified below.

## Setup

The numbers are those of the paper's table I, case 1. `case.py --case N`
writes the other cases.

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 67 mm × 4.19 mm; the plates at $x = 0$ and $x = L$, periodic in $y$ |
| Grid | 128 × 8 cells of $L/128 = 0.523$ mm; the benchmark is 1-D along $x$ |
| Voltage | $450\sin(2\pi f t)$ V on the left plate, $f = 13.56$ MHz; the right plate grounded |
| Walls | absorb electrons and ions; no secondary emission |
| Gas | helium, $9.64\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ at 300 K, a uniform background |
| Collisions | electrons: elastic, two excitations (19.82 and 20.61 eV), ionisation; He⁺: isotropic and backward scattering; the benchmark's cross sections |
| Start | a uniform plasma of $2.56\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, electrons at 30 000 K, ions at 300 K |
| Particles | 64 per cell in each species: the benchmark's 512 per 1-D cell over the 8 rows; ion mass $6.67\times10^{-27}$ kg |
| Time step | $1/(400 f) = 1.84\times10^{-10}$ s, the same for the ions |
| Run | 512 000 steps (1280 RF periods); profiles every 8 steps of the last 32 periods; no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | the direct solve of a strip |
| Cost | about 2 min on one NVIDIA A100; 2.2 GB of output |

## Running

```bash
python examples/discharges/ccp_helium/case.py runs/ccp_helium --run
python examples/discharges/ccp_helium/analyze.py runs/ccp_helium
```

`case.py` writes `runs/ccp_helium/case.toml`, copies the cross sections
beside it and, with `--run`, runs `build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names
another binary). HEXAPIC looks for the cross sections from the directory it
is started in, so `--run` starts it in the run's directory. `case.toml`
beside the script is the same case: run it from this directory. `--case N`
selects the benchmark's case, `--seed N` the seed, `--periods N` a shorter
run. `analyze.py` takes one run or several runs with different seeds, prints
the numbers and writes `results.json` and the figure into the run's
directory. `--live` makes `case.py` write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ
shows during the run.

## Deviations from the benchmark

- **The energy of an ionisation.** The benchmark gives the two electrons
  half of the remaining energy each. HEXAPIC gives the primary a uniformly
  drawn share, and has no switch for it. This changes the result, see below.
- **Two dimensions.** The benchmark is 1-D. Here it is a strip of 8 cells
  with a 2-D field, with the benchmark's number of particles per unit of
  $x$. A run with the charge spread evenly over $y$ (`[debug] field_1d`)
  gives the same density within the scatter of the seeds.
- **Particle shape.** The benchmark's codes weight linearly (CIC). The
  default build of HEXAPIC uses the quadratic shape (TSC). Both builds were
  run and agree, see the table below.
- **Cross sections.** The tables are not from the paper's supplement but
  from a public copy, checked against the paper's figure 1 and table III.
  HEXAPIC resamples each table to 1024 points uniform in the logarithm of
  the energy. The references at the end of this page have the details.
  The built-in helium tables of HEXAPIC are not used: they differ from the
  benchmark's by factors of 2 to more than 10 (the excitation).
- **Masses.** The gas atom has HEXAPIC's built-in helium mass,
  $6.695\times10^{-27}$ kg, 0.4 % above the benchmark's ion mass.
  The constants are CODATA 2018, not 2006.
- **The average.** The profiles are averaged over every 8th step of the
  last 32 periods (1601 profiles), not over every step, and the densities
  are those of the cells: a node's density is the mean of its two cells, and
  the two wall nodes are left out.

## Results

### Ion density against the benchmark

The benchmark's result is the ion density $n_i(x)$ averaged over the last 32
RF periods. The paper compares a code's profile with the mean $\bar n_i$ and
the standard deviation $\sigma_i$ of a long run of its code E by

$$
X^2 = \sum_j \frac{\left[n_i(x_j) - \bar n_i(x_j)\right]^2}{\sigma_i(x_j)^2} ,
$$

summed over the nodes $x_j$. For case 1, 95 % of the values of $X^2$ of
statistically equal runs lie between 55 and 303. Here $\bar n_i$ and
$\sigma_i$ are read from the paper's figure 7, on 127 of the 129 nodes.

![The time-averaged ion density across the gap and enlarged around the middle: HEXAPIC lies 11 % above the paper's curve in the middle, on the curve of the 1-D check with uniform sharing](../../_examples/discharges/ccp_helium/figures/density.png)

*The time-averaged ion density of case 1: the long run of code E of the
paper with its standard deviation (band), HEXAPIC (the mean of 5 seeds, TSC
build), and the 1-D check with the two ways of sharing the energy of an
ionisation (the mean of 3 seeds each).*

| Case 1 | $n_i(L/2)$ [$10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$] | against the paper | $X^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper, table III; long run of code E | 0.140; $0.1405 \pm 0.0024$ | | 55 to 303 |
| HEXAPIC, TSC (default build), 5 seeds | $0.1561 \pm 0.0014$ | +11.2 % | $1160 \pm 230$ |
| HEXAPIC, CIC build, 5 seeds | $0.1555 \pm 0.0028$ | +10.7 % | $970 \pm 450$ |
| HEXAPIC, CIC, 1-D field, 1 seed | 0.1555 | +10.7 % | 942 |
| HEXAPIC, CIC, 8 times the particles, 1 seed | 0.1584 | +12.8 % | 1100 |
| 1-D check, equal sharing, 3 seeds | $0.1390 \pm 0.0011$ | −1.1 % | 81 to 186 |
| 1-D check, uniform sharing, 3 seeds | $0.1588 \pm 0.0019$ | +13.1 % | 1100 to 1360 |

The uncertainties are the standard deviations over the seeds; $\pm 0.0024$
is the paper's standard deviation of a single run.

HEXAPIC's density is 11 % above the benchmark in the middle of the gap, six
standard deviations of a single run, and its $X^2$ is far outside the
accepted range. The paper's codes agree with each other to better than
0.5 %. The difference does not depend on the particle shape, on the field
being 2-D, or on the number of particles.

The cause is the sharing of the energy of an ionisation. A 1-D PIC-MCC
script written for this test
(`reference/pic1d/pic1d.py`) follows the
paper's prescription. With the benchmark's equal sharing it reproduces the
benchmark: its $X^2$ is inside the accepted range for all three seeds. With
HEXAPIC's uniformly drawn share, and nothing else changed, its density
rises by 14 % and lies on HEXAPIC's profile. A uniform share produces more
slow electrons, which stay in the middle of the discharge and lower its
electron temperature, and with it the speed at which the ions leave.
HEXAPIC is 2 % below the 1-D check with uniform sharing, about two standard
errors; this remainder is not explained further.

At the higher pressure of case 2, one run (CIC build, one seed) gives
$0.847\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ in the mid-plane against the paper's
$0.828\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ ($\pm 0.005$ for a single run), a
difference of +2.2 %. Its largest difference from the paper's profile is
3.0 % of the mid-plane density. This is still outside the paper's
statistical bounds.

### Other quantities of the paper

The paper's table III also gives the ion current density $J_i$ at the
electrodes, the power to the ions per unit area,
$S_i = \int \langle J_i E \rangle\,\mathrm{d}x$, and the electron temperature
$T_e$ in the mid-plane, all time averaged.

| Case 1 | HEXAPIC, TSC, 5 seeds | Paper, table III |
|---|---|---|
| $J_i$ [$\mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}$] | $0.220 \pm 0.001$ | 0.219 |
| $S_i$ [$\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$] | $91.1 \pm 0.3$ | 90.6 |
| $T_e$ [eV] | $7.52 \pm 0.05$ | 9.36 |

The ion current and the power to the ions agree with the paper to 0.5 %:
the ionisation rate of the discharge as a whole is the benchmark's. The
electron temperature in the middle is lower, which goes with the higher
density there. The two temperatures are not defined in the same way:
HEXAPIC's is the mean of the cells' temperatures, each taken about the
cell's mean velocity, and the paper does not state its definition. The
definition accounts for an estimated 0.4 eV of the 1.8 eV (the energy of
the RF drift and the few particles of a cell). The power to
the electrons is not compared: the profiles hold the velocities half a time
step from the field, which is not accurate enough for
$\langle J_e E \rangle$.

## References

The benchmark: {cite:t}`turner2013`. The paper was read as its open copy,
arXiv:1211.5246 (the text and the figures as the authors' source files). The
table and figure numbers below are those of that copy. The paper's electronic
supplement (the cross-section tables and the result files) could not be
fetched; nothing here is from it directly.

### From the paper (`turner2013/`)

- The case (`../case.py`): every physical and numerical parameter is from
  table I. The model (absorbing electrodes without emission, a uniform gas at
  300 K, isotropic scattering, the energy of an ionisation shared equally,
  tables interpolated linearly, linear weighting, no subcycling) is from the
  paper's sections II and III.
- `table.csv`: the ion density and the electron temperature in the mid-plane,
  the powers to the electrons and to the ions and the ion current density at
  the electrodes (table III), and the accepted ranges of the statistic $X^2$
  (table IV), copied by hand.
- `ion_density_case<N>.csv`: the time-averaged ion density of the five codes
  A to E, and the mean and the standard deviation of the long run of code E,
  read from the upper panels of figures 7 to 10. The figures are gnuplot PostScript files
  and hold the curves as lines between integer coordinates, about 3300 × 2400
  of them across a plot: a value is exact to 0.05 % of the plot's range. The
  long run's points are marked on 128 of the nodes (cases 2 to 4: every
  second or fourth node). As a check of the reading, the statistic $X^2$ of
  the five codes computed from these files is 231, 176, 309, 219 and 57 for
  case 1; the paper's table IV has 240, 192, 328, 242 and 57 (one of the 129
  nodes is not marked in the figure).

`turner2013/extract.py` wrote all of these but `table.csv`, on 2026-11-17:

```bash
curl -L -o src.tar.gz https://arxiv.org/e-print/1211.5246 && mkdir arxiv && tar -xzf src.tar.gz -C arxiv
git clone https://github.com/BLAST-WarpX/warpx-data        # commit 3033347
python extract.py arxiv warpx-data/MCC_cross_sections/He
```

### The cross sections (`turner2013/cross_sections/`): second hand

The benchmark prescribes Biagi's compilation, version 7.1, for the electrons
(elastic momentum transfer, two excitations, ionisation) and Phelps's
isotropic and backward parts for He⁺ + He, as tables in the paper's
supplement. The tables used here are **not from the supplement** but from
the [warpx-data](https://github.com/BLAST-WarpX/warpx-data) repository
(`MCC_cross_sections/He`, commit 3033347, licence CDLA-Permissive-2.0),
whose `README.md` names the same origins: Biagi's MAGBOLTZ 7.1 for the four
electron processes and Phelps's database for the two ion processes. They
are tabulated in steps of 0.01 eV up to 750 eV. Checks made against the
paper:

- The six curves of the paper's figure 1 agree with the tables to 0.1 % (the
  median of the ratio over each curve; `extract.py` prints it).
- The largest electron collision frequency of the tables gives
  $\nu_e \Delta t = 0.0158$ for case 1, which is the paper's table III.
- The two ion tables follow Phelps's expressions to $5\times10^{-6}$,
  with the centre-of-mass energy $\varepsilon$ in eV:
  $\sigma_\mathrm{iso} = 7.63\times10^{-20}\,\varepsilon^{-1/2}\ \mathrm{m^2}$
  and
  $\sigma_\mathrm{back} = 10^{-19}\,(\varepsilon/1000)^{-0.15}(1 + \varepsilon/1000)^{-0.25}(1 + 5/\varepsilon)^{-0.15}\ \mathrm{m^2}$.
  Evaluated at $10^4$ eV, the upper end of the paper's figure 1, they give
  $\nu_i \Delta t = 0.0069$ for case 1; the paper's table III has 0.00688.
  The supplement's ion tables therefore reach further than these (750 eV),
  which no ion of the benchmark does: the voltage is at most 450 V.

`extract.py` thins the tables: it drops rows as long as linear interpolation
between the remaining ones reproduces each dropped row to $10^{-4}$ of its
value. The files:

- `He_turner2013.txt`: the four electron processes in the LXCat download
  format, for `lxcat_file`. The energies lost are 19.82, 20.61 and
  24.587 eV.
- `i_He_elastic.dat`, `i_He_charge_exchange.dat`: the isotropic and the
  backward part, under the file names HEXAPIC reads from `data_dir`.
- `e_He_elastic.dat`, `e_He_excitation.dat`, `e_He_ionization.dat`,
  `e_He_double_ionization.dat`: HEXAPIC reads these names from `data_dir`
  as well. The run does not use them: the electron processes come from the
  LXCat file, and the last one (He⁺ → He²⁺, not a process of the benchmark)
  is zero.

### The 1-D check (`pic1d/`)

`pic1d.py` is a 1-D PIC-MCC code of 200 lines in numpy, written for this
example to measure what the energy sharing of an ionisation does to the
benchmark's result (see the README). It follows the paper's prescription
and reads the warpx-data tables in full. `case1.csv` holds the ion densities
of its runs of case 1 with the benchmark's equal sharing and with a
uniformly drawn share, three seeds each, run on 2026-11-17 as

```bash
python pic1d.py warpx-data/MCC_cross_sections/He equal_seed1.csv --sharing equal --seed 1
```

The null-collision method: {cite:t}`vahedi1995`.
