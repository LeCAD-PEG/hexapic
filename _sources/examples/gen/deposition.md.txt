# Ion deposition in a trench

A substrate at $-100$ V is covered by a dielectric layer with a trench. The
wall opposite to it stands for the edge of a plasma sheath: it emits ions
and electrons. The ions fall through the 100 V onto the layer and into the
trench, and the electrons turn back. The case has a dielectric region,
dielectric walls inside the domain, a biased wall and two emitters. It is
taken from XOOPIC and run with both codes. The two codes treat the
dielectric differently, so only the flow above the layer and in the trench
is compared, and only before the layer has charged.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 1.6 µm × 3.2 µm, periodic in $x$; 32 × 64 cells of 50 nm |
| Substrate | the bottom wall, at $-100$ V, absorbing |
| Layer | a dielectric region with the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r = 10$, 1 µm thick |
| Trench | 0.5 µm wide and 0.9 µm deep, in the middle; 0.1 µm of the layer is left under it. Its two sides and its bottom are dielectric walls. |
| Top wall | at 0 V; it emits ions and electrons downwards over its whole width |
| Ions | $1.5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$ (0.24 A per metre of depth), 0.025 eV, 40 electron masses |
| Electrons | $5\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$ (0.08 A/m), 5 eV |
| Collisions | Monte Carlo collisions of both species with argon at 10 mTorr, with the cross sections multiplied by 40 |
| Particles | 1000 per macroparticle: 75 ions and 25 electrons per step |
| Time step | $5\times10^{-14}$ s |
| Run | 1000 steps (50 ps), output every 10 steps, no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Cost | about 4 s on one NVIDIA A100 |

An ion crosses the domain in about 120 steps. The run is eight such
transits: the flow is steady, and the charge that the ions bring changes the
potential by less than 0.1 V. The charge of the ions in flight changes it by
less than 0.01 V, so the potential is that of the empty geometry.

![Two maps of the domain with the outline of the layer and the trench: the potential with its equipotential lines, and the ion density](../../_examples/walls/deposition/figures/case.png)

*The potential and the ion density of the steady state in HEXAPIC, with the
outline of the layer. In HEXAPIC the ions are also inside the layer (see
below).*

## Running

```bash
python examples/walls/deposition/case.py runs/deposition --run
python examples/walls/deposition/analyze.py runs/deposition
```

`case.py` writes `runs/deposition/case.toml` and, with `--run`, runs
`build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml` beside
the script is the same case, for a run without Python. `analyze.py` prints
the numbers below and writes `results.json` and the two figures into the
run's directory. Given several runs, it prints their mean and standard
deviation.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--run` | run HEXAPIC on the case |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |
| `--seed N` | the seed of the random numbers |
| `--xoopic` | write the case as an XOOPIC deck, `case.inp` |

## Results

### Ion flow and potential against XOOPIC

The numbers are means over the outputs from step 300 on. HEXAPIC is run with
five seeds:

```bash
for s in 1 2 3 4 5; do
    python examples/walls/deposition/case.py runs/deposition_$s --seed $s --run
done
python examples/walls/deposition/analyze.py runs/deposition_?
```

Both codes are reduced in the same way. The ions are counted in each cell,
with the sums of their $v_y$ and of their $|v|^2$. The ion current through a
row of cells of the trench is

$$
I = \frac{q\,w}{\Delta y} \sum v_y ,
$$

with the charge $q$ of an ion, the weight $w$ of a macroparticle, the cell
size $\Delta y$ and the sum over the ions in that row. An even flow of
$1.5\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$ over the width of the trench is 0.075 A/m.

![Four panels: the ions above the layer against time, the ion density above the layer and the ion current down the trench against y, and the difference of the two potentials against y](../../_examples/walls/deposition/figures/comparison.png)

*Top: the ions above the layer against time, and their density against the
height $y$. Bottom left: the ion current through each row of cells of the
trench, with the range of the five HEXAPIC seeds. Bottom right: the
difference of the potentials of the two codes along two vertical lines.*

| Steady state | HEXAPIC, 5 seeds | XOOPIC | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ions above the layer (macroparticles) | $7106 \pm 1$ | 7224 | $-1.6$ % |
| Ion current into the trench, top row of cells [A/m] | $0.0730 \pm 0.0015$ | 0.0728 | $+0.2$ % |
| Ion current onto the bottom of the trench, lowest two rows [A/m] | $0.0679 \pm 0.0011$ | 0.0657 | $+3.4$ % |
| Mean energy of the ions in those two rows [eV] | $98.189 \pm 0.002$ | 96.33 | $+1.9$ % |
| Potential at the mouth of the trench [V] | $-90.93$ | $-90.03$ | 0.90 V |
| Potential on the surface of the layer, between two trenches [V] | $-94.62$ | $-94.44$ | 0.18 V |
| Potential one cell above that point [V] | $-93.36$ | $-92.17$ | 1.19 V |
| Potential at the bottom of the trench [V] | $-99.88$ | $-99.90$ | 0.02 V |
| Electrons in the domain (macroparticles) | $158.6 \pm 0.6$ | 157.5 | $+0.7$ % |

The ion density above the layer, the current into the trench and the number
of electrons agree within 2 %. The potentials differ by at most 1.2 V of
100 V. The remaining differences have four causes.

**The surface of the layer.** HEXAPIC sets the permittivity on the nodes, and
a cell edge between a node of the layer and a node outside takes a value in
between. The layer therefore acts about half a cell thicker than in XOOPIC,
which changes the permittivity exactly at the surface. The field above the
layer is 43 V/µm, so this shifts the potential there by about 1 V. The
difference is largest one cell above the surface and falls linearly to zero
at the top wall.

**The emitter.** XOOPIC starts each particle on the wall. HEXAPIC starts it
half a cell inside the domain, where the potential is already $-1.07$ V. The
ions of HEXAPIC miss this part of the fall, and the two rows of cells at the
emitter hold about 200 fewer of them. This gives the 1.6 % in the number of
ions.

**The collisions.** In XOOPIC the charge exchange with the gas takes
2.8 eV from the mean energy of the ions at the bottom of the trench: without
its `MCC` block, XOOPIC gives 99.18 eV there, 1.0 eV above HEXAPIC as the
emitter explains. In HEXAPIC no ion and no electron collided in this run.
The keys `scale_electron` and `scale_ion` have no effect in the current
version: the collision rates in the log are those of the cross sections
without the factor 40.

**The walls of the trench.** XOOPIC absorbs an ion that reaches a side of the
trench. In HEXAPIC a dielectric wall inside the domain does not stop the
particles: an ion that reaches a side moves on into the layer, and an ion
inside the layer can enter the trench. HEXAPIC has 3.4 % more current at
the bottom of the trench. This is the likely cause; it was not measured
separately.

### What is not compared

XOOPIC stops the particles at the surface of a dielectric region and keeps
their charge there. In HEXAPIC a dielectric region changes the permittivity
only: the ions cross the layer and are absorbed by the substrate, and no
charge stays on the layer or in the trench. The totals over the domain
therefore differ:

| Steady state, whole domain | HEXAPIC, 5 seeds | XOOPIC |
|---|---|---|
| Ions (macroparticles) | $8739 \pm 1$ | 7652 |
| Kinetic energy [J/m] | $6.06\times10^{-11}$ | $4.17\times10^{-11}$ |
| Field energy [J/m] | $2.840\times10^{-8}$ | $3.380\times10^{-8}$ |

The two field energies are also not the same integral. Computed from the
two potentials with one formula, $\int \tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r
E^2\,\mathrm{d}A$, they are $3.02\times10^{-8}$ and $3.00\times10^{-8}$ J/m.

In XOOPIC the layer charges: at the end of this run its surface holds about
$6\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$ and its potential has risen by 0.07 V. Over
a run a thousand times longer this charge would change the flow, and HEXAPIC
would not follow it. The comparison holds for the uncharged layer only.

XOOPIC's numbers come from its stored run in `reference/xoopic/`.
`analyze.py --xoopic DIR` takes them from a new run of XOOPIC instead (see
`reference/SOURCE.md`).

## References

The case follows XOOPIC's deck `input/deposition.inp`
{cite:p}`verboncoeur1995`. That deck has a domain of 1.5 µm × 5 µm with
30 × 100 cells, a time step of $10^{-13}$ s and $10^{4}$ particles per
macroparticle. Here the domain is 1.6 µm × 3.2 µm with 32 × 64 cells of the
same size, the time step is $5\times10^{-14}$ s and a macroparticle holds
$10^{3}$ particles. The trench, the layer, the voltage, the emitted currents
and the gas are those of the deck. There is no theory in this example.

### XOOPIC's run (`xoopic/`)

- `case.inp`: the deck XOOPIC ran, as `python case.py --xoopic` writes it.
  Besides the case it has three `Diagnostic` blocks, which make XOOPIC write
  the phase space of both species and the potential every 10 steps. Its
  first lines note that XOOPIC's `DielectricRegion` stops the particles.
- `history.csv`: at every 10th of 1000 steps, XOOPIC's field and kinetic
  energy (J/m) and its numbers of ions and electrons from the `ENERGY_CSV`
  lines it prints (which list the species in the reverse order of the
  deck), and the ions above the layer from the phase-space files.
- `ions.csv`: the steady state, the mean over the steps 300 to 990, by row
  of cells: the ion density above the layer, and in the trench the ion
  current, the mean ion energy and the number of ions.
- `potential.csv`: the mean potential over the same steps along $x = 0$ and
  along the axis of the trench.

XOOPIC 2.70 (github.com/rinku-mishra/xoopic, commit 3fa6670) with the
`ENERGY_CSV` lines added to `otools/diagn.cpp`
([Running XOOPIC](../../docs/verification/codes.md#running-xoopic) gives the patch and the build), run on
2026-11-17 as

```bash
mkdir -p runs/deposition_xoopic
python examples/walls/deposition/case.py runs/deposition_xoopic --xoopic --run > runs/deposition_xoopic/xoopic.log
```

which is `xoopic -nox -i case.inp -s 1000 -exit` in that directory, about
3 s on one CPU core. The three CSV files were then written by

```bash
python examples/walls/deposition/analyze.py runs/deposition_? --xoopic runs/deposition_xoopic --store
```

XOOPIC draws the same random numbers in each run. The number in the README
for XOOPIC without collisions is from the same deck without its `MCC` block
and without the `collisionModel` lines of the two species; that run is not
stored.
