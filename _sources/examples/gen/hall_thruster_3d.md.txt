# Hall thruster in 3-D

<video class="hx-video" controls loop muted playsinline preload="metadata" poster="../../_static/videos/hall_thruster_3d.jpg" src="../../_static/videos/hall_thruster_3d.mp4"></video>

A Hall thruster ionizes a gas in an annular channel and accelerates the ions
with an axial electric field. A radial magnetic field holds the electrons
back, so that the field can stand in the plasma: the electrons drift around
the channel instead, in the E×B direction. This case is a small thruster of
the anode-layer type (TAL), which has a short channel with metal walls. It
is simulated in 3-D with its channel, a central cathode and the plume in a
grounded vacuum chamber. The 2-D [LANDMARK](landmark.md) benchmark
covers the axial and the azimuthal direction of such a discharge; this case
adds the geometry, the curved magnetic field and the plume. It has no theory
and no other code to compare with, so the results below describe what the
run gives.

## Setup

The axis of the thruster is along $x$ and passes through the middle of the
$y$–$z$ plane. Radii are measured from this axis.

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 20 mm × 24 mm × 24 mm; all six sides are walls |
| Grid | 160 × 192 × 192 cells of 0.125 mm |
| Back wall, $x = 0$ | the anode at 300 V, a ring between the radii 3 and 6 mm; the rest of the wall is grounded |
| Channel | from the anode to the exit at $x = 2$ mm, between the radii 3 and 6 mm |
| Body | the inner pole (radius below 3 mm), the outer pole (6 to 7 mm) and the mount (beyond 7 mm), all from $x = 0$ to 2 mm. They are one grounded conductor, made of 316 boxes, so the walls of the channel are metal at 0 V. |
| Magnetic field | the field in the gap between the two pole tips, which cover $x = 0.5$ to 2 mm. It is computed in `case.py` from a magnetic scalar potential and given as $B_x$ and $B_r$ on a grid of $x$ and the radius. At the middle radius of the channel, 4.5 mm, $B_r$ is 12 mT at the anode, 20 mT at its peak at 1.3 mm, 18 mT at the exit and 4 mT at 5 mm. |
| Ionization | no neutral gas and no collisions. Pairs of an electron (10 eV) and a Xe⁺ ion (0.5 eV) are created in the channel between $x = 0.25$ and 1.75 mm, at a rate with a sine profile along $x$. The source gives an ion current of $100\ \mathrm{A/m^2}$ over the cross-section of the channel, 8.48 mA. |
| Cathode | a square of 1.5 mm on the axis at $x = 6$ mm. Each step it emits as many electrons (5 eV) as the anode collected net. |
| Chamber | grounded walls at $x = 20$ mm and at 12 mm from the axis; they absorb |
| Particles | none at the start; $5\times10^{3}$ particles per macroparticle; about $4.5\times10^{6}$ ions and $3.7\times10^{6}$ electrons in the steady state. The ions are moved every 10th step. |
| Time step | $10^{-11}$ s |
| Run | 365 000 steps (3.65 µs), output every 5000 steps |
| Field solver | [GMG3D](../../docs/solvers/gmg3d.md), the 3-D multigrid; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Build | the default build (quadratic particle shape, single precision) |
| Cost | about 1 h 50 min on one NVIDIA A100 |

The thruster is scaled down in density. Its plasma is about ten times
thinner than that of a real thruster, at most $9\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.
The Debye length is then about ten times longer, and a cell of 0.125 mm
resolves it (1.2 Debye lengths at $10^{17}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and 20 eV).

## Running

```bash
python examples/thrusters/hall_thruster_3d/case.py runs/hall_thruster_3d
build/HEXAPIC runs/hall_thruster_3d/case.toml > runs/hall_thruster_3d/run.log
python examples/thrusters/hall_thruster_3d/analyze.py runs/hall_thruster_3d
```

`case.py` writes `runs/hall_thruster_3d/case.toml`. It needs numpy and scipy
to compute the magnetic field. `analyze.py` reads the series and the log of
the run, `run.log`, which has the currents of the walls and of the body. It
prints the numbers below and writes `results.json` and the three figures
into the run's directory.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--steps N` | the number of steps |
| `--every N` | an output every `N` steps |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |

| Option of `analyze.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--last N` | average the last `N` outputs (default 20) |
| `--plane X` | the plane of the thrust and of the beam, in mm (default 15) |
| `--log FILE` | the log of the run (default `run.log` in the run's directory) |

## Results

The discharge is steady from about 2 µs. All numbers below are means over
the last 20 outputs, from 2.70 to 3.65 µs. Over this window the discharge
current changes by 1.1 % and the number of ions by 0.8 %. Profiles "at the
middle radius" are means around the axis at the radius 4.5 mm, and "on the
axis" means within 0.6 mm of it.

### The discharge

![The potential with the magnetic field lines near the channel, and the ion density in the plane through the axis](../../_examples/thrusters/hall_thruster_3d/figures/maps.png)

*Left: the potential and the lines of the magnetic field in the upper half
of the plane through the axis, near the channel. Right: the ion density in
the whole plane. The body is grey, the anode orange and the cathode green.*

The plasma in the channel is at the potential of the anode, up to 307 V.
The potential falls where the magnetic field is strong: at the middle radius
it is 295 V at $x = 1$ mm, 177 V at the exit, 82 V at 3 mm and 28 V at
5 mm. The axial field $E_x$ peaks at $1.47\times10^{5}$ V/m at 1.8 mm, just
inside the exit. The electron temperature peaks at 29 eV at 1.3 mm, where
the magnetic field has its peak.

In the crossed fields the electrons drift around the axis. At the exit
their mean azimuthal velocity is $5.1\times10^{6}$ m/s, which is 0.71 of the
E×B drift of the mean fields, $7.2\times10^{6}$ m/s.

![Profiles along x of the potential, the ion density, the ion energy and the azimuthal drift of the electrons](../../_examples/thrusters/hall_thruster_3d/figures/profiles.png)

*Profiles along $x$ at the middle radius of the channel and on the axis:
the potential, the ion density, the energy of the ions' mean axial velocity,
$\tfrac{1}{2} M v_x^2$, and the azimuthal drift of the electrons with the
E×B drift. The dotted line is the exit of the channel. The E×B drift is
drawn where $B_r$ is above a tenth of its peak.*

### The plume

The ions are not magnetized. They fall through the potential drop at the
exit and reach 240 to 255 eV at 15 to 18 mm, which is 0.8 of the 300 V of
the anode. Ions that leave the channel towards the axis cross there and form
a central jet. Beyond 5 mm the ion density on the axis is 5.5 to 9.7 times
the density at the middle radius.

The thrust is the momentum flux of the ions through a plane, the beam
current $I_b$ is their current through it, and the divergence is the rms
angle of their mean velocity to the axis, weighted by their flux. The
discharge current $I_d$ is the current of the anode.

| | at $x = 6$ mm | at $x = 15$ mm |
|---|---|---|
| Thrust [µN] | 65 | 51 |
| Beam current $I_b$ [mA] | 2.82 | 2.03 |
| $I_b / I_d$ | 0.28 | 0.20 |
| Mean energy of the beam [eV] | 187 | 231 |
| Divergence [°] | 28 | 19 |

The values fall with the distance because the plume is wider than the
chamber: 1.30 mA of ions reach its side walls. The divergence at 15 mm is
that of the ions that are left.

### The currents

![The discharge current and the ion currents to the poles and to the walls of the chamber over the run](../../_examples/thrusters/hall_thruster_3d/figures/currents.png)

*The discharge current, the ion current to the two poles and the ion current
to the walls of the chamber over the run. The black line is the ion current
that the source creates.*

| Current, mean over the window | [mA] |
|---|---|
| Discharge current $I_d$ | $9.99 \pm 0.11$ |
| Ions created by the source | 8.48 |
| Ions to the inner pole | 1.95 |
| Ions to the outer pole | 3.46 |
| Ions to the anode | 0.10 |
| Ions out of the channel | 2.97 |
| Ions to the far wall of the chamber | 1.71 |
| Ions to the side walls of the chamber | 1.30 |

Of the 8.48 mA of ions that the source creates, 5.41 mA are lost to the
poles and 2.97 mA leave the channel. The loss is large because the walls of
the channel are grounded metal 1.5 mm from a plasma at about 300 V: the ions
that are born where $E_x$ is still weak are pulled to the walls. The
currents collected by all surfaces add up to 8.52 mA, 0.5 % more than the
source creates, as the number of ions is still falling slowly.

## References

The case has no theory and no data from other codes to compare with.

Two parts of its model are those of the 2-D benchmark of
{cite:t}`charoy2019`, which the [LANDMARK example](landmark.md)
reproduces: the ionization is an imposed source of electron–ion pairs with
a sine profile along the axis, and the cathode emits in each step the charge
that the anode collected. The geometry, the magnetic field and the scaling
of the thruster are this case's own.
