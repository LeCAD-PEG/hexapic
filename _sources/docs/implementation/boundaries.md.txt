# Boundaries and objects

A wall does two things. It sets a condition for the field, and it decides
what happens to the particles that reach it. Walls are the sides of the
domain or segments of them, defined in
[`[[boundary]]`](../user-guide/case-toml.md#boundary). Objects inside the
domain are conductor and dielectric regions, defined in
[`[[region]]`](../user-guide/case-toml.md#region).

## Walls

| Type | Field | Particles |
|---|---|---|
| `grounded` | $\phi = 0$ | absorbed |
| `biased` | $\phi(t) = V + A\sin(2\pi f t + \varphi)$ | absorbed |
| `floating` | floats with the collected charge | absorbed, charge collected |
| `dielectric` | collected charge as a surface charge, or $\partial\phi/\partial n = 0$ | absorbed, charge collected |
| `open` | $\phi = 0$ | removed |
| `reflective` | $\partial\phi/\partial n = 0$ | reflected |
| periodic, set in `[grid] periodic` | periodic | wrapped |

A floating wall is a Neumann wall. The charge it collects enters the field
solve as a surface charge. The wall settles at the potential where the
fluxes onto it balance, which is its sheath potential. Floating walls of one
`group` share one potential.

A dielectric wall that accumulates charge is an insulating surface. Like a
floating wall it is a Neumann wall, and the charge it has collected enters
the field solve as a surface charge. This is the dielectric of XOOPIC with
`QuseFlag = 1`. With `charge_accumulating = false` the collected charge is
discarded and the wall is a plain Neumann wall.

A floating or dielectric wall can cover part of a side. The other nodes of
that side are grounded. A reflective wall always covers a whole side.

## Conductor and dielectric regions

A conductor region is solid. Its nodes are fixed in the field solve, and the
particles that reach it are absorbed. A conductor can be biased or floating:

- A biased conductor has a `voltage`, and its nodes are held at that
  potential.
- A floating conductor has no `voltage`. Its potential follows from the
  charge it has collected and from its capacitance. It is solved together
  with the field in each step, as described in
  [Solvers](../solvers/index.md#conductors-and-dielectrics). Regions of one
  `group` share one potential.

A dielectric region sets the permittivity of its nodes. Particles pass
through it.

## Surface emission

A wall can emit particles in response to the particles it absorbs. This is
set in [`[[boundary.surface]]`](../user-guide/case-toml.md#boundarysurface)
and models secondary electron emission and recycling, where ions return as
neutrals.

For each absorbed particle of `species_in`, the wall emits `yield` particles
of `species_out`. A `yield` below 1 is a probability. The emitted particles
leave the wall with the temperature `temperature_eV`. With `flux_maxwellian`
they are drawn from a flux Maxwellian. With `energy_eV` they all have the
same energy and a cosine-law direction.

## Particle injection

A beam, set in `[[beam]]`, injects particles from a segment at a constant
current $I$. Each injection creates
$I\,\Delta t_\mathrm{inj} / (\lvert q\rvert\,w)$ macroparticles of weight
$w$, where $\Delta t_\mathrm{inj}$ is `every` × $\Delta t$.

A volumetric source, set in `[[source]]`, creates particles in a box at a
given rate per volume. The rate is uniform, or it has a cosine or sine
profile in x.
