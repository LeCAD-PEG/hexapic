# Landau damping

A Langmuir wave is an oscillation of the electrons of a warm plasma against
the ions. Without any collisions it still damps: the electrons that move
nearly as fast as the wave take energy from it {cite:p}`landau1946`. A fluid
model has no such damping, so the case tests that the particles carry the
velocity distribution correctly. The damping is measured over a few
e-folds, until the wave sinks into the noise of the particles. The case
therefore needs many particles.

## Setup

The thermal speed is $v_{th} = \sqrt{T_e/m_e}$, the Debye length
$\lambda_D = v_{th}/\omega_{pe}$, and $k$ is the wave number.

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 0.934 mm × 0.117 mm, periodic in $x$ and $y$; one wavelength long, $k\lambda_D = 0.5$ |
| Grid | 64 × 8 cells of 14.6 µm ($0.196\,\lambda_D$); the problem is 1-D along $x$ |
| Electrons | $10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, Maxwellian, $T_e = 1$ eV: $v_{th} = 4.19\times10^{5}$ m/s, $\lambda_D = 74.3$ µm |
| Ions | H⁺, $10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$, cold, moved every 10th step |
| Start | 8 slabs along $x$; the electrons of slab $j$ drift at $u_0 \sin(k x_j)$ along $x$, where $x_j$ is the middle of the slab and $u_0 = 0.05\,v_{th}$ |
| Particles | 131072 per cell in each species, $6.7\times10^{7}$ in all; the electrons start on the ions' positions |
| Frequency | $\omega_{pe} = 5.64\times10^{9}$ rad/s |
| Time step | $1.77\times10^{-11}$ s ($\omega_{pe}\,\Delta t = 0.1$) |
| Run | 300 steps ($\omega_{pe} t = 30$), output every 2 steps, no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Cost | about 80 s on one NVIDIA A100, 7 GB of its memory, 4 GB of output |

A load has one drift velocity, so the sine of the start is built from slabs.
The first Fourier mode of this staircase is a standing Langmuir wave. Its
higher modes have $k\lambda_D \ge 3.5$ and damp within a plasma period.

Most of the output is the particles of the first step, which HEXAPIC always
writes. The log warns `omega_pe dt = 0.283`: this check adds the densities of
the eight loads of the electrons, although they fill different slabs. The
value of the case is 0.1.

## Running

```bash
python examples/basic_plasma/landau_damping/case.py runs/landau_damping --run
python examples/basic_plasma/landau_damping/analyze.py runs/landau_damping
```

`case.py` writes `runs/landau_damping/case.toml` and, with `--run`, runs
`build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml` beside
the script is the same case, for a run without Python. `analyze.py` prints
the damping rate and the frequency and writes `results.json` and the figure
into the run's directory.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--run` | run HEXAPIC on the case |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |
| `--seed N` | the seed of the random numbers |
| `--particles-per-cell N` | the particles per cell of each species (131072) |
| `--drift X` | the largest drift of the start, $u_0 / v_{th}$ (0.05) |

## Results

### Damping rate and frequency against theory

A wave $\propto e^{i(kx - \omega t)}$ in Maxwellian electrons over cold ions
has the complex frequency $\omega$ that solves the dispersion relation

$$
1 + \frac{1}{k^2\lambda_D^2}\left[1 + \zeta Z(\zeta)\right] - \frac{\omega_{pi}^2}{\omega^2} = 0 ,
\qquad \zeta = \frac{\omega}{\sqrt{2}\,k\,v_{th}} ,
$$

where $Z$ is the plasma dispersion function {cite:p}`fried1961` and
$\omega_{pi}$ the plasma frequency of the ions. The root with the smallest
damping is the Langmuir wave. Its real part is the frequency and its
imaginary part the damping rate $\gamma < 0$. `analyze.py` computes the root
with Newton's iteration, with $Z(\zeta) = i\sqrt{\pi}\,w(\zeta)$ from the
Faddeeva function $w$ of SciPy. For $k\lambda_D = 0.5$ and fixed ions it is
$\omega = (1.4157 - 0.1534\,i)\,\omega_{pe}$. With protons it is
$(1.4158 - 0.1533\,i)\,\omega_{pe}$, and the comparison uses this value.

The start gives a standing wave with $E_x \propto \sin(kx)$. `analyze.py`
takes the sine part of the first Fourier mode of $E_x$. The damping rate is
the slope of a line through the logarithm of the peaks of its magnitude. The
frequency follows from the mean distance of its zero crossings. The cosine
part of the mode has no wave. Its root mean square over the second half of
the run is the noise floor.

The fit window starts at $\omega_{pe} t = 5$. Before that, the other roots
of the dispersion relation contribute: the next one,
$(1.79 - 1.14\,i)\,\omega_{pe}$, is below 1 % of the wave from there on. The
window ends where the line through the peaks comes down to 5 times the noise
floor.

![The magnitude of the first mode of the electric field against time on a logarithmic axis: the peaks follow the line of the theory through the fit window and reach the noise floor after it](../../_examples/basic_plasma/landau_damping/figures/damping.png)

*The magnitude of the first mode of $E_x$ in one run. The black line has the
slope of the theory. The dashed line is the noise floor.*

The noise differs from seed to seed, so the result is the mean over six
seeds:

```bash
for s in 1 2 3 4 5 6; do
    python examples/basic_plasma/landau_damping/case.py runs/landau_damping_$s --seed $s --run
done
python examples/basic_plasma/landau_damping/analyze.py runs/landau_damping_?
```

| | $\gamma / \omega_{pe}$ | $\omega / \omega_{pe}$ |
|---|---|---|
| HEXAPIC, 6 seeds | $-0.156 \pm 0.009$ | $1.405 \pm 0.013$ |
| Theory | $-0.1533$ | 1.4158 |

The damping rate is 1.6 % above the theory in magnitude and the frequency
0.75 % below it. The numbers after $\pm$ are the standard deviations of
single runs. The mean of six runs is uncertain by 2.3 % in $\gamma$ and by
0.4 % in $\omega$, so the damping rate agrees within the uncertainty, and
the frequency is low by two standard errors.

Two things limit the measurement, and they pull in opposite directions.

The noise floor ends the fit window. Electrons on the ions' positions give
no field at the start, but the thermal motion mixes the positions within a
few plasma periods. The floor is then 0.4 % of the wave's first amplitude
with 131072 particles per cell, and 0.9 % with 32768: it falls with the
square root of the number of particles. The window covers about 3 e-folds
of the wave, 9 or 10 peaks. At its end the noise changes a peak by up to
20 %.

A larger wave would rise further above the noise, but it is no longer
linear. The electrons that move with the wave are trapped in it. The
damping then weakens and the frequency falls. Single runs with the same
seed show this:

| $u_0 / v_{th}$ | 0.05 | 0.1 | 0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\gamma / \omega_{pe}$ | $-0.151$ | $-0.147$ | $-0.119$ |
| $\omega / \omega_{pe}$ | 1.397 | 1.377 | 1.346 |

At $u_0 = 0.05\,v_{th}$ the density changes by 1.7 %. The trend of the
table suggests that the low frequency of the case is partly this effect and
not only noise. A smaller wave with more particles would decide it, at a
higher cost.

## References

The collisionless damping of a Langmuir wave: {cite:t}`landau1946`. The
plasma dispersion function $Z$: {cite:t}`fried1961`. The loading of a warm
plasma and the noise of the particles: {cite:t}`birdsall1991`.

The reference values are not taken from a paper. `analyze.py` computes the
root of the dispersion relation for the case's $k\lambda_D$ and mass ratio
with Newton's iteration, with $Z$ from `scipy.special.wofz`. For
$k\lambda_D = 0.5$ and fixed ions it gives
$\omega = (1.41566 - 0.15336\,i)\,\omega_{pe}$. The case has no data from
other codes.
