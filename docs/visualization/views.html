The views#

A run opens with a default layout of views that depends on its dimension. This page describes how to arrange the views and what each of them shows.

Arranging views#

To add a view, click + in a tab bar or + add a view in the side bar. The menu groups the views by what they show, in the same way as ParaView does, and describes each one briefly.

Group

Views

Maps

The field map and, for a 3-D run, the 3-D view

Plots

Profile, line cut, probe, region, energies and phase space

Data

The table

For a 3-D run, a field map shows one plane and the 3-D view shows the whole box.

To split a view, click ◫ to add a view on its right or ⊟ to add one below it. You can also drag a tab. If you drop it on the edge of another view, that view is split. If you drop it on the middle, the two views are stacked as tabs. Views can be closed and shown again later.

The layout, the settings of the views and the theme are stored in the browser. The buttons ↶ and ↷ undo and redo a change to them. The shortcuts are Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z.

The side bar#

Open the side bar with the « tab on its edge or with the \ key. Its sections follow the docks of ParaView.

Section

Contents

Views

The open views. Closed views are listed with their settings, so that you can show them again.

Data

The quantities you can put into the selected view: the fields, the quantities and phase spaces of each species, the derived quantities and the expressions

Properties

The settings of the selected view

Information

The run, and for the selected view the array with its shape, minimum, maximum and mean at the current step

Layout

The session. save writes the views, their settings, the layout, the expressions, the step and the theme into a file. load restores a session from a file.

Field maps#

A field map shows a quantity on the grid as an image with a colour bar. You can draw contour lines, a grid and the computational mesh over it. It can also show the conductors and dielectrics of the case, which are read from hexapic_used.toml.

A field map normally shows the current step. It can instead show a statistic over a range of steps: the mean, the minimum, the maximum or the standard deviation at every point.

Field lines#

The Field lines section draws the lines of E or B in the plane of the map. This also works for a plane of a 3-D run. The lines are evenly spaced and have arrows along the field. A slider sets their density from ×0.25 to ×3, and the lines are traced again when you release it.

Before the lines are traced, the field is smoothed over about half the spacing between the lines. A noisy field from particles then shows its large-scale structure.

Colours#

The colour maps are viridis, inferno, magma, plasma, cividis, turbo, greys and blues. The diverging maps are RdBu, cool–warm, PuOr and BrBG. A map can be inverted. It can be continuous or have 4 to 32 levels, and its scale can be linear or logarithmic.

The colour range is taken from the whole run, from the current step or from the visible part of the map, or you set it by hand. For a diverging map the range is symmetric about 0.

Phase spaces#

A phase-space view shows one species. Position selects the position axis, which is x or y, and in 3-D also z. Velocity selects the velocity component: vx, vy or vz.

The data are the histograms that HEXAPIC writes at every output step when [output] phase_space is on. They have 256 × 512 bins, and there is one for every pair of axis and component. If a series has no histograms, the viewer builds the phase space from the particles, at the latest step that has particles. A series from an older version of HEXAPIC has only x–vx and x–vy.

Scale sets the colour scale of the counts per bin. It is logarithmic by default and can be switched to linear.

Line plots#

View

Shows

Profile

The mean, minimum or maximum over the other axis, plotted along x or y. Step 0 is drawn dashed for comparison.

Line cut

The values along a line. Draw the line with ╱ on a field map.

Probe

The value at a point over the run. Place the point with ✛ on a field map.

Region

The mean, minimum, maximum, standard deviation or integral inside a rectangle, over the run. Draw the rectangle with ▭ on a field map.

Energies

The energies over the run

Every plot has settings for its y axis and for its line. The axis can be linear, logarithmic or symlog. Its range comes from the run or from the step, or it is fixed. The line has a colour and a width and can show its points.

Tables#

A table lists the nodes or the cells at the current step, one row per point. The columns are i, j, x, y and every quantity that is defined there, with the values of the series. You can sort the table by a column. The row under the mouse is marked on the field maps.

Derived quantities and expressions#

The viewer computes some quantities itself, in the browser, at each step:

  • |E|

  • |B|, if the run has a [magnetic_field]

  • the net charge density divided by e

  • the flux n Vx of each species

  • the Debye length of the electrons

You can also define a quantity of your own with + expression, like the calculator of ParaView. An expression combines other quantities by their names. It can also use the coordinates x, y and z in metres, the time t in seconds, and the constants pi, qe, me, mp, eps0, mu0, kB, c and nan. In a 2-D run z is 0.

sqrt(Ex^2 + Ey^2)
electrons_density - Hplus_density
where(phi > 10, electrons_T, nan)

An expression then behaves like any other quantity. You can show it in a field map, a profile, a probe or a table, and you can export it.

The 3-D view#

A 3-D run opens with a 3-D view. It shows a quantity in the box of the domain in one of three ways: as three slices, as a volume or as an isosurface. It also draws the axes of the box, the solids and the outlines of the slices. Derived quantities and expressions can be shown as well.

When the mouse is over the view, the viewer shows the value under it with its x, y and z. This is the value on a slice, the value of the isosurface, or the largest value along the ray through the volume. Over a solid it shows the name of the solid.

Field lines draws the lines of E or B through the box, in the same way as a field map draws them in its plane. The lines are evenly spaced, have arrows along the field and a slider for their density. Lines farther away are fainter. The lines are drawn over the image, so a solid or an isosurface does not hide them.

Other views of a 3-D run#

A field map of a 3-D run shows one plane: xy, xz or yz. The plane is placed at a fixed position or linked to the slice of the 3-D view. The line cut, the probe and the region of that map lie in this plane.

A region can also be a box. The box is the rectangle extended to a depth along the normal of the plane, and its integral is taken over the volume.

A profile is taken from the middle z slice or from the whole volume. It runs along x, y or z and reduces over the other two axes. A table shows the middle z slice.

Performance#

A run with [output] performance has a performance.jsonl file. For such a run the run menu contains a Performance item. You open the run menu by clicking the name of the run in the header, marked with ▾.

The item opens a panel over the views. The panel shows where the time of a step goes: on which rank, on which device and on which link. To return to the views, choose Visualisation in the menu or press Esc.

Part

Shows

Figures

The time and the rate of a step, the nanoseconds per particle and step, the balance between the ranks, the share of MPI, the busy time of the devices, the traffic, and the run time or the expected end of the run

Timeline

One output step. For each rank it shows the queues of the field, of each species and of the collisions, with every phase as a bar at the time it ran.

Hardware

The hosts and their devices, with the ranks on them coloured by a metric. Each link is as wide as its bytes per step.

Domain

The boxes of the ranks coloured by the metric, and the links between them. For a 3-D run it shows one layer in z.

Over the run

The time of each phase per step. It can show the mean over the ranks, the slowest rank or the imbalance, which is the maximum divided by the mean. A click selects the output step that the other parts show.

The panel shows the same output step as the app, so playing the run or moving the timeline also moves the panel. Hover over any element to see its values. The first sample is not shown, because it contains the setup with the autotuning of the solver.

The run menu has two more items. toml shows the case as it ran, as tables, and Log shows the log of the run. Both open under the header and have a filter.

Export#

The properties of every view have an export of its data. The export contains the values of the series, and not the 16-bit or 8-bit frames that the views draw. Data are exported as CSV with the units in the column names. For a field map or a phase space you can also export the array of the current step as .npy. The array is float64 with the index order [y, x].

PNG saves the views as they appear on the screen. MP4, or the key R, records the whole run as a video with one frame per step.
