# Electron beam between grounded plates

A cold electron beam enters an empty box through its left wall, crosses it
between two grounded plates and leaves through the right wall. The charge of
the beam lowers the potential on its axis and pushes the electrons apart, so
the beam widens on its way. The case has an emitter, absorbing conductors
and two dielectric walls, and no collisions. The same case is run with
XOOPIC, from which it is taken.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 2 m × 1 m, 48 × 24 cells of 41.7 mm |
| Plates | $y = 0$ and $y = 1$ m: grounded, absorbing |
| Left wall | dielectric, absorbing, $\partial\phi/\partial x = 0$; it keeps the charge it collects |
| Right wall | dielectric, absorbing, $\partial\phi/\partial x = 0$; the charge it collects is discarded |
| Emitter | the left wall between $y = 0.292$ m and $y = 0.708$ m |
| Beam | electrons at $10^{6}$ m/s along $x$ (2.84 eV), no thermal spread, 5 µA per metre of depth |
| Particles | 4000 electrons per macroparticle, 39 macroparticles per step, about 15 500 in the steady state |
| Time step | 5 ns: the beam moves 0.12 cells per step |
| Run | 2000 steps (10 µs, five transits of the box), output every 10 steps, no charge smoothing |
| Field solver | [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid; relative tolerance $10^{-5}$ |
| Cost | about 10 s on one NVIDIA A100 |

![The electron density of the beam between the two plates, from the emitter on the left wall to the right wall](../../_examples/walls/xybeam/figures/beam.png)

*The mean electron density of the steady beam in HEXAPIC, and the walls. The
stripes are explained under Results.*

## Running

```bash
python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam --run
python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam
```

`case.py` writes `runs/xybeam/case.toml` and, with `--run`, runs
`build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml` beside
the script is the same case, for a run without Python. `analyze.py` prints
the numbers below and writes `results.json` and the two figures into the
run's directory. Given several runs, it prints their mean and standard
deviation.

| Option of `case.py` | |
|---|---|
| `--run` | run HEXAPIC on the case |
| `--live` | write the frames that HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run |
| `--seed N` | the seed of the random numbers (the emitter draws the $y$ of each electron) |
| `--xoopic` | write the case as an XOOPIC deck, `case.inp` |

## Results

### Steady state against XOOPIC

The beam fills the box after one transit, 2 µs. The numbers below are means
over the second half of the run. HEXAPIC is run with five seeds:

```bash
for s in 1 2 3 4 5; do
    python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam_$s --seed $s --run
done
python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam_?
```

The rms width $\sigma_y$ of the beam is taken in each column of cells, from
the number of electrons in its cells. A beam of the emitter's width $w$ and
uniform density has $\sigma_y = w/\sqrt{12} = 0.1203$ m.

![Four panels: the electrons in the box and the field energy against time, the rms width of the beam and the potential on its axis against x, for HEXAPIC and XOOPIC](../../_examples/walls/xybeam/figures/comparison.png)

*Top: the number of electrons in the box and the field energy against time.
Bottom: the rms width of the steady beam and the potential on its axis,
$y = 0.5$ m. The band of the five HEXAPIC seeds is narrower than the line.*

| Steady state | HEXAPIC, 5 seeds | XOOPIC | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rms width in the last column of cells [m] | $0.1753 \pm 0.0003$ | 0.1765 | $-0.7$ % |
| Potential on the axis at $x = 1$ m [V] | $-0.1078 \pm 0.0001$ | $-0.1079$ | $-0.1$ % |
| Potential on the axis at $x = 2$ m [V] | $-0.1011 \pm 0.0001$ | $-0.1008$ | $+0.3$ % |
| Potential on the axis at $x = 0$ [V] | $-0.1047 \pm 0.0001$ | $-0.1114$ | $-6.0$ % |
| Electrons in the box (macroparticles) | $15\,485 \pm 0.4$ | 15 617 | $-0.8$ % |
| Kinetic energy [J/m] | $(2.8222 \pm 0.0001)\times10^{-11}$ | $2.8512\times10^{-11}$ | $-1.0$ % |
| Field energy [J/m] | $(4.676 \pm 0.001)\times10^{-13}$ | $4.758\times10^{-13}$ | $-1.7$ % |

The beam widens from 0.120 m to 0.175 m in both codes, and from the middle
of the box to the exit the potentials agree within 0.3 %.

The two codes differ at the emitter. XOOPIC starts each electron on the wall
and moves it by a random part of a time step. HEXAPIC starts every electron
half a cell inside the box, at $x = 20.8$ mm. The beam of HEXAPIC is
therefore half a cell shorter, which is 1.0 % of the box: it holds 0.8 %
fewer electrons and 1.0 % less kinetic energy. The first half cell holds no
charge, so the potential at the left wall is 6 % higher, and the field
energy is 1.7 % lower. The electrons of one step also start at the same $x$
and stay together. A column of cells holds the electrons of 8.33 steps, so
some columns hold eight groups and some nine. These are the stripes in the
density above, and the ripple in the width of HEXAPIC near the exit.

The field energy of XOOPIC still rises by 0.4 % over the second half of the
run. That of HEXAPIC is constant within 0.2 %. The cause was not looked
for.

XOOPIC's numbers come from its stored run in `reference/xoopic/`.
`analyze.py --xoopic DIR` takes them from a new run of XOOPIC instead (see
`reference/SOURCE.md`).

## References

The case follows XOOPIC's deck `input/xybeam.inp` {cite:p}`verboncoeur1995`.
That deck has a box of 1 m × 1 m with 20 × 20 cells and an emitter of 0.4 m.
Here the box is 2 m × 1 m with 48 × 24 cells of 1/24 m, and the emitter is
0.417 m wide (the nodes 7 to 17). The beam, the walls and the time step are
those of the deck. There is no theory in this example.

### XOOPIC's run (`xoopic/`)

- `case.inp`: the deck XOOPIC ran, as `python case.py --xoopic` writes it.
  Besides the case it has two `Diagnostic` blocks, which make XOOPIC write
  the electrons' phase space and the potential every 10 steps.
- `history.csv`: XOOPIC's field energy, kinetic energy (J/m) and number of
  macroparticles at every 10th of 2000 steps, from the `ENERGY_CSV` lines it
  prints.
- `profiles.csv`: the steady state, the mean over the steps 1000 to 1990:
  the rms width of the beam in each column of cells (from the phase-space
  files) and the potential on the axis $y = 0.5$ m (from the potential
  files).

XOOPIC 2.70 (github.com/rinku-mishra/xoopic, commit 3fa6670) with the
`ENERGY_CSV` lines added to `otools/diagn.cpp`
([Running XOOPIC](../../docs/verification/codes.md#running-xoopic) gives the patch and the build), run on
2026-11-16 as

```bash
mkdir -p runs/xybeam_xoopic
python examples/walls/xybeam/case.py runs/xybeam_xoopic --xoopic --run > runs/xybeam_xoopic/xoopic.log
```

which is `xoopic -nox -i case.inp -s 2000 -exit` in that directory, about
6 s on one CPU core. The two CSV files were then written by

```bash
python examples/walls/xybeam/analyze.py runs/xybeam_? --xoopic runs/xybeam_xoopic --store
```

XOOPIC draws the same random numbers in each run.
