# GMGa3: multigrid in 2-D

GMGa3 is the multigrid solver of every 2-D case. It solves the operator
described in [Solvers](index.md#the-operator); its keys are in
[`[solver.multigrid]`](../user-guide/case-toml.md#solvermultigrid-2-d).

## Krylov iteration

The outer iteration is a flexible conjugate-gradient method, preconditioned
by one V-cycle. It uses the Polak–Ribière form of $\beta$:

$$
\beta_k = \frac{\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{\mathsf{T}}\left(\mathbf{r}_{k+1} - \mathbf{r}_k\right)}
               {\mathbf{z}_k^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{r}_k} .
$$ (eq:beta)

The solve stops when the relative residual reaches `tolerance`. With
`stagnation_stop` it also stops when the residual no longer decreases. With
`phi_extrapolation`, each solve starts from an extrapolation of the last two
solutions.

## The V-cycle

The V-cycle applies damped-Jacobi sweeps before and after the coarse
correction. It uses full-weighting restriction and bilinear prolongation.
Sometimes one axis cannot be coarsened further, because the mesh is
anisotropic or because a rank runs out of cells along that axis. The solver
then coarsens only the other axis. This is called semi-coarsening.

## Boundaries

Periodic, Dirichlet and Neumann walls can be combined freely on each axis.
The Neumann operator is the symmetric half-cell form. If all walls are
periodic or all are Neumann, the operator is singular. The solver then
enforces the compatibility condition and projects the null space out of the
solution.

## Several ranks

The multigrid hierarchy is distributed over the ranks down to an aggregation
level. Below that level, every rank gathers the right-hand side and solves
the coarse problem itself, ending with a dense direct solve. The coarse solve
therefore costs one collective MPI call per cycle.

On the distributed levels the solver uses a communication-avoiding cycle. It
exchanges a deep halo twice per level, where a standard cycle exchanges a
halo after every sweep. The result is the same. Data is transferred through
host memory, so GPU-aware MPI is not needed.

## One rank

On one rank the small coarse levels run in a single kernel. The convergence
check is a flag on the device. The host can therefore enqueue a fixed budget
of iterations without waiting for each one.

## Autotuning

At setup the solver times several of its choices on the actual problem.
These are the depth of the hierarchy, the bottom solve, the damping and the
number of sweeps of the smoother, and the size of the fused levels. It prints
each choice together with the `--set` option that fixes it. This is the
output for the two-stream case of
[First run](../get-started/first-run.md):

```text
[distMG] autotune (singular operator, timed): 9 of 9 levels, bottom_sweeps 4  [to pin: --set solver.multigrid.aggregate=1 --set solver.multigrid.bottom_sweeps=4]
[distMG] smoother autoselect (timed): omega 0.8, sweeps 2  [to pin (a reproducible run): --set solver.multigrid.omega=0.8 --set solver.multigrid.sweeps=2]
[distMG] fused coarse cycle (timed): levels <= 4096 nodes in one block  [to pin: --set solver.multigrid.fuse_nodes=4096]
```


## Accuracy and performance

The solver is second-order accurate for every combination of boundary
conditions. {numref}`fig:solver-scaling` compares it with HYPRE-PFMG, PETSc
and AMReX MLMG on LUMI-G, on up to 128 AMD MI250X GCDs. The
[Performance](../performance/scaling.md) page has the numbers.
