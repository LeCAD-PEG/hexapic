Boundaries and objects#

A wall does two things. It sets a condition for the field, and it decides what happens to the particles that reach it. Walls are the sides of the domain or segments of them, defined in [[boundary]]. Objects inside the domain are conductor and dielectric regions, defined in [[region]].

A floating wall is a Neumann wall. The charge it collects enters the field solve as a surface charge. The wall settles at the potential where the fluxes onto it balance, which is its sheath potential. Floating walls of one group share one potential.

A dielectric wall that accumulates charge is an insulating surface. Like a floating wall it is a Neumann wall, and the charge it has collected enters the field solve as a surface charge. This is the dielectric of XOOPIC with QuseFlag = 1. With charge_accumulating = false the collected charge is discarded and the wall is a plain Neumann wall.

A floating or dielectric wall can cover part of a side. The other nodes of that side are grounded. A reflective wall always covers a whole side.

Conductor and dielectric regions#

A conductor region is solid. Its nodes are fixed in the field solve, and the particles that reach it are absorbed. A conductor can be biased or floating:

  • A biased conductor has a voltage, and its nodes are held at that potential.

  • A floating conductor has no voltage. Its potential follows from the charge it has collected and from its capacitance. It is solved together with the field in each step, as described in Solvers. Regions of one group share one potential.

A dielectric region sets the permittivity of its nodes. Particles pass through it.

Surface emission#

A wall can emit particles in response to the particles it absorbs. This is set in [[boundary.surface]] and models secondary electron emission and recycling, where ions return as neutrals.

For each absorbed particle of species_in, the wall emits yield particles of species_out. A yield below 1 is a probability. The emitted particles leave the wall with the temperature temperature_eV. With flux_maxwellian they are drawn from a flux Maxwellian. With energy_eV they all have the same energy and a cosine-law direction.

Particle injection#

A beam, set in [[beam]], injects particles from a segment at a constant current \(I\). Each injection creates \(I\,\Delta t_\mathrm{inj} / (\lvert q\rvert\,w)\) macroparticles of weight \(w\), where \(\Delta t_\mathrm{inj}\) is every × \(\Delta t\).

A volumetric source, set in [[source]], creates particles in a box at a given rate per volume. The rate is uniform, or it has a cosine or sine profile in x.
