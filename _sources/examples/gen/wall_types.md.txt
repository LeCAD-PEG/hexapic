# Wall types

A square box holds a uniform argon plasma, and eight variants of the case
put different walls around it and different objects into it. A wall sets a
condition for the potential and decides what happens to a particle that
reaches it; a conductor inside the box is held at a voltage or floats. The
example shows each type at work on the same plasma, and it checks the
potentials that the types prescribe. The case has no collisions and no
source, so the plasma only loses particles.

## Setup

| | |
|---|---|
| Domain | 15.0 mm × 15.0 mm |
| Grid | 64 × 64 cells of 0.235 mm, half a Debye length of the start |
| Plasma | electrons at 4 eV and Ar⁺ at 0.05 eV, uniform at $10^{15}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ at the start; the ions are moved every 10th step |
| Particles | 32 per cell in each species |
| Collisions, sources | none |
| Time step | $5\times10^{-11}$ s ($\omega_{pe}\,\Delta t = 0.089$) |
| Run | 20 000 steps (1 µs), output every 200 steps |
| Field solver | the direct [strip solver](../../docs/solvers/strip.md) for the five variants with nothing inside the box, [GMGa3](../../docs/solvers/gmga3.md), the 2-D multigrid, for the three with a conductor; `type = "auto"` chooses |
| Cost | about 10 s a variant with the strip solver and 1.5 to 3 minutes with the multigrid, on one NVIDIA A100 |

The variants, chosen with `--walls`:

| Variant | Walls | Inside the box |
|---|---|---|
| `grounded_box` | four grounded walls | |
| `open_box` | open at both $x$ ends, grounded at both $y$ ends | |
| `reflective_box` | grounded at both $x$ ends, reflective at both $y$ ends | |
| `ac_edge` | the wall at $x = 0$ biased at $V\sin(2\pi f t)$ with $V = 20$ V and $f = 5.25$ MHz, the others grounded | |
| `biased_edge` | the wall at $x = 0$ held at $-20$ V, the others grounded | |
| `biased_probe` | four grounded walls | a square conductor of 8 × 8 cells in the middle, held at $-20$ V; a dielectric slab of 4 cells with $\varepsilon_r = 4$ across the box, between the wall at $x = 0$ and the conductor |
| `floating_probe` | four grounded walls | the same conductor, floating |
| `two_probes` | four grounded walls | two floating conductors of 8 × 8 cells at a quarter and at three quarters of the box along $x$, each with its own potential |

A grounded and an open wall both hold $\phi = 0$ and both remove the
particles that reach them. A reflective wall returns the particles and
sets $\partial\phi/\partial n = 0$. Particles pass through the dielectric
slab; it only changes the permittivity.

## Running

```bash
for w in grounded_box open_box reflective_box ac_edge biased_edge biased_probe floating_probe two_probes; do
    python examples/walls/wall_types/case.py runs/wall_types/$w --walls $w --run
done
python examples/walls/wall_types/analyze.py runs/wall_types
```

`case.py` writes `runs/wall_types/<variant>/case.toml` and, with `--run`,
runs `build/HEXAPIC` on it (`--hexapic` names another binary). `case.toml`
beside the script is the variant `grounded_box`, for a run without Python.
`analyze.py` takes the directory that holds the variants' runs, prints the
numbers below and writes `results.json` and the figure there. `--seed N`
sets the seed of a run, and `--live` makes `case.py` write the frames that
HEXAPIC / VIZ shows during the run.

## Results

![Eight maps of the potential at the end of the run, one for each variant, with the walls drawn by type and the conductors and the dielectric slab outlined: the plasma is a few volts positive in every box, the biased edge and the biased probe are at -20 V, and the box with the AC edge is at +23 V](../../_examples/walls/wall_types/figures/walls.png)

*The potential at the last step of each variant. The AC edge is at its
crest of $+20$ V there.*

| Variant | Ions left | Electrons left | Plasma potential [V] | Probes [V] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `grounded_box` | 60.2 % | 54.7 % | 5.7 | |
| `open_box` | 60.2 % | 54.6 % | 5.7 | |
| `reflective_box` | 77.7 % | 74.2 % | 5.9 | |
| `ac_edge` | 48.9 % | 36.9 % | 14.3 | |
| `biased_edge` | 53.7 % | 46.0 % | 5.5 | |
| `biased_probe` | 44.6 % | 35.5 % | 3.2 | $-20$ (held) |
| `floating_probe` | 51.8 % | 45.5 % | 4.1 | $-0.18 \pm 0.10$ |
| `two_probes` | 46.0 % | 39.4 % | 3.9 | $-0.12 \pm 0.09$ and $-0.25 \pm 0.10$ |

The plasma potential is the largest potential inside the box, and it and
the potentials of the floating probes are means over the last ten outputs
(0.1 µs, half a period of the AC edge); the scatter is the standard
deviation of those ten. The numbers are those of one run of each variant.

What the walls do to the plasma:

- In every box the electrons leave first, and the plasma settles a few
  volts above the walls, which holds the rest back. A box of 32 Debye
  lengths loses about 40 % of its ions in 1 µs through the sheaths at its
  four walls.
- The open and the grounded box behave alike, as their walls prescribe
  the same potential and remove the same particles.
- With reflective walls at both $y$ ends the plasma is lost only through
  the two $x$ walls, and about half as many particles leave.
- A wall or a conductor at $-20$ V draws a wider sheath and more ions.
  The slab changes the field between the wall and the probe, and the
  particles cross it.
- The AC edge drives the potential of the whole plasma up and down: at the
  crest the plasma is 3.5 V above the edge. This box loses the most
  electrons.
- A floating probe settles within 0.3 V of the grounded walls. The walls
  and the probe face the same plasma, and in a steady state both collect
  as many electrons as ions. The two probes of `two_probes` are mirror
  images and float at the same potential within the scatter.

### Checks of the prescribed potentials

Where a type prescribes a potential exactly, `analyze.py` compares it with
the output at every node of the wall or conductor and at every output
step.

| Check | Largest difference [V] |
|---|---|
| Grounded and open walls at 0 V, all variants | 0 |
| `biased_edge`: the wall at $-20$ V | 0 |
| `ac_edge`: the wall at $20\,\sin(2\pi f t)$ V over 5.25 periods | $1.6\times10^{-5}$ |
| `biased_probe`: every node of the conductor at $-20$ V | 0 |
| `floating_probe`, `two_probes`: difference between the nodes of a conductor | 0 |

The difference at the AC edge is the rounding of the single-precision
output. The corner nodes belong to two walls and are left out; a corner
between a biased and a grounded wall has the biased wall's potential.

A box with four reflective walls is not among the variants. Changed to
that by hand (the two grounded walls of `reflective_box` set to
`reflective`), the case ran and kept all 131 072 particles of each species
over 2000 steps.

## References

The case is compared with no theory and has no data from other codes. The
checks of the README are the conditions that the wall types and the
conductors prescribe (the `case.toml` reference, `[[boundary]]` and
`[[region]]`): the potential of a grounded, open or
biased wall, and one potential over a conductor.
